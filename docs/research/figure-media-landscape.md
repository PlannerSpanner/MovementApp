# Figure media landscape: is a stick figure the right medium?

Researched 2026-11-12. Every bullet carries its source. "Vendor" marks a company describing itself or a rival. Items I could not confirm are in the last section, not here.

## 1. How leading apps produce demonstrations

- **Down Dog** (real video, generated sequencing). Co-founder Carlos Ormachea: all of Down Dog was powered by about 18 hours of recorded content, which the software recombines into "an endless stream of unique classes". Two founders, launched August 2015. The article is circa 2017, so the 18 hours is a floor today. https://seattleyoganews.com/down-dog-app-yoga/
- **pliability (ex-ROMWOD)** and **GOWOD**: filmed video. GOWOD says "over 300 mobility videos" with an on-screen timer. https://www.gowod.app/concept , https://yogaia.com/blog/mobility-apps
- **Fitbod**: 1,600+ exercises, "professionally filmed personal trainers". https://fitbod.me/about-fitbod-exercises
- **JEFIT**: re-shot its library in HD in 2023 and added a secondary angle. https://www.jefit.com/blog/major-feature-update-new-2023-hd-video-exercise-demonstrations
- **Hevy**: 400+ exercises (official); a testimonial on its site praises "illustrations and animations". https://www.hevyapp.com/features/exercise-library/
- **Muscle and Motion**: 3D anatomy animation, based in Israel, used by 300+ universities. It does license content, in two forms: a Deep Link License (your users click through into the M&M app) and a Commercial Embed License (their videos embedded by Vimeo link or embed code). Both are annual, per product, price by quote only (sales@muscleandmotion.com). Neither hands over files or rigs, so neither fits an offline timer with a phase-colored background. https://www.muscleandmotion.com/?p=24050 , https://www.muscleandmotion.com/about-us
- **FitCraft** (vendor, post dated 2026-03-22): real-time 3D model per exercise that the user rotates and zooms, "no pre-recorded videos and no fixed camera angles". Its argument is that a single video angle hides knee tracking or squat depth. It names no engine, mocap source or exercise count. https://getfitcraft.com/blog/3d-exercise-demonstrations
- **Seven**: illustrated/3D animated instructor characters, also on Apple Watch. https://apps.apple.com/us/ipad/story/id1290833110 , https://pushinteractions.com/app-review-7-minute-workout-seven
- **Nike Training Club**: trainer-led produced video; one review notes users disliked being pushed to video when self-guided programs went away. https://reviewed.com/health/content/nike-training-club-review-workout-app
- **User sentiment** is anecdotal and split. Complaints target crude animation ("blocky stick-person", "two- or three-frame animations", "hard to see details on the exercises and the form"); praise goes to animated guides that pair with tap-for-cues text. No systematic review data found. https://www.stuff.tv/?p=349378 , https://dailytarheel.com/article/2016/10/workout-apps-tested , https://reviewed.com/health/best-right-now/the-best-workout-apps

## 2. Live Activity / Dynamic Island / lock screen limits

All from Apple's current docs unless noted.

- UI is SwiftUI + WidgetKit inside a **widget extension**. No video, no web view, no custom render loop. https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/displaying-live-data-with-live-activities
- **Animation**: "Animations in widgets and Live Activities have a maximum duration of two seconds." They fire only when content state changes (content transitions for text, images and SF Symbols; opacity/move/slide/push transitions). On Always-On displays the system performs no animations. https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit/animating-data-updates-in-widgets-and-live-activities
- **Data**: static plus dynamic data, including each update, cannot exceed 4 KB combined. So no image bytes in the state; reference bundled assets by name.
- **Images**: asset resolution must be smaller than or equal to the presentation size, otherwise "the system might fail to start the Live Activity". Lock-screen height above 160 pt may be truncated. Limited-width compact views must fit a 28 pt circle.
- **Duration**: active up to 8 hours, then up to 4 more on the Lock Screen (12 maximum). A 12-minute session is nowhere near this.
- **Sandbox**: no network or location inside the activity. The app starts it in the foreground and may update or end it from the background.
- **Update rate**: Apple publishes no number. An Apple forum reply says they "purposely discourage and throttle" now-playing-style update frequency; push updates have a dynamic budget. A timer should use system-rendered timer text rather than per-second updates. https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/822595 , https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/731715
- **Capacitor**: no official plugin. Community options: `@capgo/capacitor-live-activities` (JSON layout from JS, image save into an App Group, a step/loop timer-sequence API), `capacitor-live-activity` (kisimedia, June 2025), `@ciabosoftwaresolutions/capacitor-live-activities` (v0.1.2, ships an extension template), and Airship's SDK. All still require a widget extension target added in Xcode with SwiftUI code; Capgo's docs say the extension needs "a compatible native layout renderer". https://capgo.app/docs/plugins/live-activities/getting-started/ , https://dev.to/kisimedia/how-to-use-ios-live-activities-in-capacitor-apps-4eno , https://www.pkgstats.com/pkg:@ciabosoftwaresolutions/capacitor-live-activities
- **Mac**: "To build iOS apps, you will need macOS"; Capacitor 8 needs Xcode 26.0+. https://capacitorjs.com/docs/getting-started/environment-setup
- **Consequence for the figure**: the lock screen can show one static pose image per movement (pre-rendered, bundled in the extension, small) or a glyph, swapped with a transition of two seconds or less at each movement change. A looping figure is not possible in any medium. The current skeleton solver can pre-render those stills headlessly; video or mocap media would need a separate still or glyph set made anyway.

## 3. Native 3D, web 3D, meshes and clips

- **SceneKit**, WWDC25 session 288, verbatim: "this year Apple is officially deprecating SceneKit across all platforms"; "This is a soft deprecation, meaning that existing applications that use SceneKit will continue to work. However, if you're planning a new app or a significant update, SceneKit is not recommended"; "Apple will only fix critical bugs"; "there's no plan to hard deprecate SceneKit". https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/288/
- **RealityKit** is the stated replacement: iOS/iPadOS/macOS/visionOS/tvOS, USD-based, `RealityView` renders "entirely virtual scenes", USD animations exposed through `AnimationLibraryComponent`. Same source.
- **Native view with Capacitor**: the established pattern is a native view under a transparent web view (camera-preview and maps plugins) or above it; small native views inline in web content are "mostly not possible", and web modals or page transitions can paint over an under-layer. https://forum.ionicframework.com/t/capacitor-plugins-limitations/238851 , https://capawesome.io/blog/capacitor-embedded-barcode-scanner/
- **WebGPU** ships by default in Safari 26 / iOS 26 and, per an Apple forum reply, in WKWebView once default-on. A WebKit bug made WebGPU unusable for one engine on iOS 26.0.1, reported fixed in 26.4 beta. three.js falls back to WebGL 2 automatically. https://web.dev/blog/webgpu-supported-major-browsers , https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/770862 , https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=301627 , https://threejs.org/manual/en/webgpurenderer
- **Meshes**:
  - MakeHuman/MPFB exports are CC0, commercial use explicitly allowed (MPFB code is GPLv3, assets CC0). https://static.makehumancommunity.org/makehuman/faq/are_makehuman_files_free.html
  - Quaternius Universal Base Characters: 6 rigged humanoids, pack page says CC0. Third-party guides claim a newer non-CC0 "QAL" license going forward; keep a copy of the license in the download. https://quaternius.com/packs/universalbasecharacters.html
  - Mixamo: free, commercial use allowed; the one prohibition is distributing raw character/animation files, so they must be baked into the app (2022 pinned FAQ; a 2026 community reply says unchanged). https://community.adobe.com/t5/mixamo-discussions/mixamo-faq-licensing-royalties-ownership-eula-and-tos/td-p/13234775
  - Ready Player Me: acquired by Netflix 2025-12-19, public services shut down 2026-01-31. Not an option. https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/19/netflix-acquires-gaming-avatar-maker-ready-player-me/
- **Clips**: Mixamo has "over 2,000 animations" with "dozens" of exercise ones (secondary source). MoCap Online's Standard License covers commercial projects up to 1M users / $1M revenue; its catalog is locomotion and combat, not mobility. Game-engine marketplaces sell gym/calisthenics mocap packs (for example a 68-clip exercise set, a 134-clip looped calisthenics pack). https://schoolofmotion.com/blog/why-mixamo-is-still-one-of-the-best-animation-resources , https://mocaponline.com/pages/licensing , https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/tricksup-sport-exercises-animation-pack-1/2637413

## 4. Rive, Lottie, Spine

- **Rive runtime**: bones, constraints including IK (strength 0-100% blends FK and IK, animatable), state machines, data binding/view models, Luau scripting. Runtimes are MIT-licensed: web/JS, iOS, Android, Flutter, React Native, C++, Unity, Unreal. https://rive.app/docs/editor/constraints/ik-constraint , https://rive.app/pricing
- **Size**: iOS runtime about 3.3 MB since v6 (docs also list ~1.67 MB download / ~4.66 MB install). Rive claims .riv is often 10-15x smaller than Lottie JSON; one team (Dub) reported the opposite for complex files. https://rive.app/docs/runtimes/runtime-sizes , https://rive.app/blog/rive-as-a-lottie-alternative
- **Programmatic authoring exists**: Rive Markup Language (RML) is an XML text representation; the Rive CLI compiles `.rml` to `.riv` (runtime) or `.rev` (editor), with `--verify`, `inspect` and `--screenshot` for headless checking, and is marketed for coding agents. The binary format is documented (major version 7). An unofficial npm writer `@stevysmith/rive-generator` also exists. https://rive.app/docs/runtimes/advanced-topic/rml , https://rive.app/docs/runtimes/advanced-topic/format , https://rive.app/agents
- **Pricing** (page read 2026-11-12): Free $0 (export carries a splash screen, 50 CLI publishes/month), Cadet $9/seat/month (no splash, 500 publishes), Voyager $32/seat, Enterprise $120/seat. Local CLI builds, previews and screenshots are unlimited without an account. https://rive.app/pricing
- **Rive in a Live Activity**: no source documents it. Apple's rules in section 2 rule out a continuously rendering runtime there regardless.
- **Lottie**: a mirror of airbnb/lottie-ios shows "archived by the owner on May 28, 2025" (see unverified list). Lottie is keyframed vector playback without a runtime rig; the same Live Activity limits apply. https://github.com/readdle/lottie-ios
- **Spine**: 2D skeletal. Runtime use requires each user of the editor to hold a license; Essential and Professional are one-time purchases valid under $500k revenue (third-party listings in 2026: Essential $69-99, Professional $369-449). spine-ios supports UIKit/SwiftUI on iOS 13+; Esoteric's forum says iOS widgets are not officially supported. https://en.esotericsoftware.com/spine-purchase , https://en.esotericsoftware.com/forum/d/25337-is-it-possible-to-integrate-spine-animation-effects-on-the-widget

## 5. Keyframes from reference video

- **BlazePose GHUM** (MediaPipe): on its yoga set the Heavy model scores 96.4 PCK@0.2 (2D, 17 COCO keypoints, one person 2-4 m away); 3D mean absolute error 36 mm (Heavy), 39 (Full), 45 (Lite). The 3D "ground truth" is GHUM fitted to 2D annotations, not mocap, and the paper notes several 3D poses fit the same 2D labels, so depth error is under-tested. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.11678
- Independent checks: accuracy "highly dependent on the camera's viewing angle as well as the performed exercise" and drops quickly off-ideal (Dill et al. 2023); monocular median RMSE 56.3 vs 30.1 with stereo fusion (Sensors 2024); bone lengths vary frame to frame and it misses a 12 degree clinical angle-error threshold (arXiv 2512.06783); on supine infants it was not competitive without fine-tuning. https://doaj.org/article/b1b1d4db2666494cacf7bcbcee8cdaa3 , https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11644880/ , https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06783v1 , https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17382v2
- **Apple Vision** `VNDetectHumanBodyPose3DRequest`: 17 joints in meters from the hip root, one skeleton for the most prominent person, optional depth input (without it, height falls back to a 1.8 m reference). Runs on Apple platforms only. https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/111241/
- **SMPL-recovery research models**: 4D-Humans/HMR2, WHAM and TRAM code is MIT, but all require SMPL body-model files, whose license says "any use for commercial purposes, is prohibited" (commercial via Meshcapade). GVHMR's own license is "educational, research and non-profit purposes only". A March 2026 developer write-up names Meta's SAM 3D Body (MHR body model, Apache 2.0) as the commercially usable alternative and reports Epic Games acquired Meshcapade in February 2026. https://github.com/shubham-goel/4D-Humans , https://github.com/yohanshin/WHAM , https://github.com/yufu-wang/tram , https://github.com/zju3dv/GVHMR/blob/main/LICENSE , https://smpl.is.tue.mpg.de/modellicense.html , https://dev.to/arkadiuss/a-3d-body-scan-for-nine-cents-without-smpl-4me1
- **Pipeline issues with sourced backing**: view-angle dependence and self-occlusion (above), inconsistent bone lengths (above). Jitter, foot sliding, loop closure and left/right swaps are well known in practice but I did not collect a citation for each.
- **Footage rights**: analysing someone else's video does not license it. Clean sources are self-recorded footage or libraries whose license permits derivative use (section 6).

No per-exercise quote found. https://beverlyboy.com/how-much-does-it-cost/how-much-does-it-cost-to-make-a-workout-video/ , https://www.london.productionattic.com/portfolio/duradiamond

  - ExerciseDB: v1 dataset is a one-time purchase; v2 claims 11,000+ exercises and 15,000+ videos by API or quote. https://exercisedb.gumroad.com/l/exercisedb
  - MuscleWiki API: 1,900+ exercises, 7,700+ videos, commercial rights on paid plans. https://api.musclewiki.com/
  - MoveKit: per-clip from $1.99, commercial license included (listing). https://alternativeto.net/software/movekit/about/
  - Workout Labs illustrations: from $15/year or $25 perpetual per illustration. https://workoutlabs.com/exercise-illustrations-licensing/
  - free-exercise-db: public domain (Unlicense), JSON plus still images, built from wrkout/exercises.json. https://github.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db
  - Freelance 3D packs: a 100-animation library for $95 on Upwork, non-exclusive app use. https://www.upwork.com/services/product/video-audio-100-workout-and-exercise-animation-library-for-apps-coaches-2011072366940827674
- **Coverage risk**: these catalogs are gym-strength heavy. None of the pages read lists qi-gong-style mobility, hip CARs or prenatal movements.
- **Background and dark mode**: video has a baked background. Transparent video on Apple platforms means HEVC with alpha (Safari since iOS 13); Safari still does not render VP9/WebM alpha (support is in the Safari 27 beta per an August 2026 guide). Encoding HEVC alpha is native on macOS; ffmpeg elsewhere may silently drop the alpha layer. https://rotato.app/blog/transparent-videos-for-the-web , https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc19/506/
- **AI-generated video**: The Markup (2026-01-21) ran 9 dance prompts across Sora 2, Veo 3.1, Kling 2.5 and Hailuo 2.3: 0 of 36 videos performed the prompted movement, and 11 of 36 had motion or consistency faults (limbs liquefying, heads rotating independently). A surgeon-rated study of Veo 3 found convincing visuals without reliable anatomical logic. https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2026/01/21/how-we-tested-ai-generated-dance-videos , https://the-decoder.com/googles-veo-3-can-fake-surgical-videos-but-misses-every-hint-of-medical-sense

## 7. Evidence on demonstration medium

- **Stick/point-light vs video**: no reliable difference. Ghorbani & Bund 2016 (41 novices, baseball pitch; video vs stick figure vs point-light): "no superiority" of point-light over video; a follow-up on a complex skill found no group differences at post-test or retention; a basketball free-throw study found none. Older results go both ways. Samples are small. https://orbilu.uni.lu/bitstream/10993/24617/1/Publikation_Ghorbani_Bund_2016_International%20Journal%20of%20Sports%20Science%20and%20Coaching.pdf , https://gymnasium.ub.ro/index.php/journal/article/view/47
- **Moving vs static**: Hoeffler & Leutner 2007 meta-analysis (26 studies): animation beats static pictures, d = 0.37 overall, d = 1.06 for procedural-motor knowledge, d = 0.76 when the animation is highly realistic (video-based). Berney & Betrancourt 2016 (140 comparisons): g = 0.226. https://www.leibniz-ipn.de/en/research/publications/instructional-animation-versus-static-pictures-a-meta-analysis , https://repositorio.uchile.cl/bitstream/handle/2250/145354/Comparing-apples-and-oranges.pdf
- **Video vs printed illustrations** (physio): video groups used more exercises and reported higher confidence in a hand/wrist RCT; a knee-osteoarthritis RCT (102 patients) found video and brochure both effective; a shoulder RCT adding animations to print found no adherence difference. Weeks et al. 2002 is cited as favoring videotape over static illustrations. https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0383/14/22/7964 , https://www.springermedizin.de/comparative-effectiveness-of-videotape-and-handout-mode-of-instr/9588860
- **Viewing angle**: mixed; several studies favor a rear view for learning a sequence, others find front and rear equal. https://www.biotech-asia.org/vol13no3/effect-of-video-modeling-demonstration-from-different-view-on-coordination-changes-of-an-unfamiliar-task-an-important-point-to-teach/ , https://ruor.uottawa.ca/items/c6828c0c-4fb6-4a08-804f-80c51a14c116/full
- **Net**: the evidence supports motion over stills and does not show that realism beats a schematic moving figure for learning a movement.
