# Strength rubrics — acceptance tests for the 11 demonstrated exercises

**Method.** Each rubric starts from Will's spec (docs/movement-reference.md, "Strength — two-workout gym reference") and is checked against outside sources fetched 2026-11-12. Where his spec differs from the textbook movement, his spec wins and the difference is recorded under "Differs from canon"; nothing here proposes changing what an exercise is.
**Source quality.** Strongest: peer-reviewed kinematics/EMG (Lee 2018 RDL, Swinton 2011 and Rivera 2022 trap bar, Schütz 2014 split squat, Fukunaga 2022 pull-apart, the 2025 pulldown EMG study) and ACE/NASM library pages read in full. Middle: StrengthLog and BarBend technique pages. Weakest: coaching-site consensus (floor-press 45° tuck, pulldown lean, incline angle), labelled as such.
**Not read.** The NSCA face-pull page and the Swinton full text returned HTTP 403; they are cited only for what their abstract/search snippet states. No Squat University, E3 Rehab, Barbell Medicine or Stronger by Science page was found for these exercises. Claims with no outside source are marked "(unsourced, from Will's spec)".
**Angle convention.** "Knee angle" is the included angle (180° = straight leg), as in Will's spec; papers that report flexion are converted (flexion = 180 − included). "Torso pitch" is degrees from vertical (0° = upright, 90° = horizontal).
**Checklist use.** Questions are answered from eight stills at phases 0, 0.125 … 0.875 of one rep. "Bottom" and "top" mean whichever stills are nearest those positions; the in-between stills are where most failures are expected, so several questions say "in every frame".

# Workout A

## A1. Goblet squat (one dumbbell)

- **Must-see positions.** (1) Top: standing tall, feet just outside shoulder width, toes slightly out; one dumbbell held vertically against the sternum, both hands cupping the upper head, elbows pointing down beneath it. (2) Bottom: hips below or level with the knees (thigh at or below horizontal), torso still tall, dumbbell still touching the chest, elbows just inside the knees, heels flat.
- **Path.** Hips and knees bend together; the hips travel down and slightly back, the knees forward and outward over the toes. The dumbbell moves straight down and up with the chest, staying over the mid-foot. At the midpoint (thighs about 45° above horizontal) the torso should already be at nearly its bottom pitch — it must not fold forward first and then drop. Feet and the hands-on-bell relationship never change.
- **Angles that matter.** Torso pitch ≤ ~25°; bottom knee angle ~65–70° (unsourced, from Will's spec). Depth: StrengthLog says "as deep as possible"; other guides say thighs at least parallel or elbows touching the inner knees, whichever comes first. Toes point where the knees travel (StrengthLog).
- **Faults.** Knees collapsing toward each other; heels leaving the floor; dumbbell drifting forward off the chest; torso pitching forward so the chest faces the floor; stopping well above parallel.
- **Readability.** The thigh line (depth) and the dumbbell-on-chest carry it. A viewer must be able to tell it from a hip hinge (torso tall, knees deeply bent) and see that it is one dumbbell at the chest, not two at the sides. Best single camera: front three-quarter (about 30–45° off straight-on). Pure side hides knee flare and the elbows-inside-knees relationship; pure front hides depth and torso pitch.
- **Constraints.** Not a press or pull; arms hold statically with palms facing each other/up under the head — neutral, acceptable for the bicep provided the figure does not read as actively curling. Right peroneal: both feet stay flat and symmetric, no rolling to the outer edge.
- **Differs from canon.** None of substance. Canon equally allows a kettlebell held by the horns; Will's is a dumbbell held vertically.
- **Checklist.**
  1. Is the dumbbell touching the chest in every frame?
  2. At the bottom, is the hip crease at or below knee height?
  3. At the bottom, are the elbows inside (not on top of or outside) the knees?
  4. Is the torso pitch about 25° or less in every frame?
  5. Are both heels on the floor in every frame?
  6. Do the knees point the same way as the toes, never inward?
  7. In the mid-descent frame, are hips and knees both visibly bent (not hips-only)?
- **Sources.** https://www.strengthlog.com/goblet-squat/ · https://breakingmuscle.com/how-to-do-the-perfect-goblet-squat/ · https://www.sussex.ac.uk/sport/documents/goblet-squat.pdf

## A2. Neutral-grip dumbbell floor press

- **Must-see positions.** (1) Top: supine on the floor, knees bent, feet flat; both arms straight and vertical, dumbbells above the chest/shoulders, handles parallel to the body's long axis (palms facing each other). (2) Bottom: the backs of both upper arms resting on the floor, angled about 45° out from the ribs; forearms vertical, wrists directly above elbows, dumbbells at roughly chest level and about a forearm's length above the floor. (3) A brief dead-stop pause there.
- **Path.** Only the arms move. Elbows travel down and slightly out to the floor, then the dumbbells press straight up and slightly inward to finish over the shoulders. Forearms stay vertical throughout — at the midpoint the elbow is at ~90°+ and the wrist is still stacked over it, not drifting toward the head or the belly. Head, ribs, pelvis and feet do not move.
- **Angles that matter.** Upper arm ~45° from the torso (coaching consensus; no peer-reviewed figure found). Range ends when the upper arms meet the floor (StrengthLog) — the upper arm never goes below the plane of the back, i.e. the elbow cannot drop behind the shoulder.
- **Faults.** Elbows flared to 90° (arms in a T); elbows bouncing off the floor with no pause; forearms tilting so the dumbbells sit over the face or belly; hips lifting or the low back arching into a bridge; palms turned toward the feet (pronated bench-press grip).
- **Readability.** The upper arm meeting the floor and the vertical forearm carry it; the dumbbell handle direction carries the grip. A viewer must be able to tell the floor is what stops the rep, and that the arms are tucked rather than in a T. Best single camera: side-on, raised slightly and shifted a little toward the head end so the bent knees never cover the arms; a pure side view hides the 45° tuck, a foot-end view hides the arms behind the knees.
- **Constraints.** AC joint: this is the defining exercise — nothing may show the elbow below the floor line/torso plane, and the arm angle must not read as wide. Grip must read neutral: handles along the body axis in every frame.
- **Differs from canon.** Canon floor press does not prescribe grip (StrengthLog is silent; many demonstrations use a pronated or semi-pronated grip). Will's is strictly neutral with a pause.
- **Checklist.**
  1. Is the whole back and pelvis on the floor in every frame?
  2. At the bottom, are both upper arms resting on the floor (not hovering, not sunk through it)?
  3. Are the forearms vertical, wrist over elbow, in every frame?
  4. Are the upper arms roughly 45° from the torso at the bottom (clearly not a T)?
  5. Are the dumbbell handles parallel to the body's long axis in every frame?
  6. At the top, are the arms straight with the dumbbells over the shoulders/chest?
  7. Do the knees stay bent with feet flat throughout?
- **Sources.** https://www.strengthlog.com/dumbbell-floor-press/ · https://alloypersonaltraining.com/dumbbell-floor-press/ · https://barbend.com/neutral-grip-dumbbell-bench-press/ (45° tuck and neutral grip are coaching consensus only)

## A3. Single-arm dumbbell row (hand and knee on bench)

- **Must-see positions.** (1) Bottom: left hand and left knee on a flat bench — hand under the shoulder, knee under the hip; right foot on the floor beside the bench; back flat and close to horizontal; right arm hanging straight down from the shoulder with the dumbbell just off the floor. (2) Top: right elbow bent and driven up and back so it is clearly above the line of the back; the dumbbell beside the lower ribs/hip; upper arm close to the side; shoulders still level.
- **Path.** The working arm only. The dumbbell travels up and slightly back in a shallow arc toward the hip, not straight up to the armpit. At the midpoint the elbow is level with the torso and the forearm still hangs roughly vertical — the forearm stays a plumb line under the elbow; the hand never leads the elbow. Torso, support arm, both legs and head are still.
- **Angles that matter.** Torso pitch ~85° (unsourced, from Will's spec; ACE says only "back flat"). ACE end point: as high as possible "without rotating your torso"; arm kept close to the body.
- **Faults.** Torso twisting open so the working shoulder rises above the other; elbow closing sharply with the dumbbell pulled to the armpit (a curl); shoulder shrugging toward the ear; back rounding or the torso rising toward upright; support hand ahead of or behind the shoulder.
- **Readability.** The working elbow relative to the back line carries it; the bench (with hand and knee on it) and the dumbbell are the equipment that must read. A viewer must be able to tell the support limbs are on a bench, not the floor, and that the elbow passes above the back. Best single camera: side-on from the working-arm side (working arm nearest the camera), optionally a few degrees toward the front so the support arm is not hidden exactly behind the working arm.
- **Constraints.** Distal bicep: palm faces the body (neutral) throughout — the handle stays parallel to the torso's long axis and must never rotate to palm-forward/up. AC joint: no issue beyond not shrugging. Right peroneal: when the right foot is the floor foot it stays flat.
- **Differs from canon.** None found; ACE's description matches. Will's "hand to lower ribs/hip, not the armpit" is a stricter path than ACE states.
- **Checklist.**
  1. Are one hand and the same-side knee on the bench in every frame?
  2. Is the back flat and near horizontal in every frame, with no change between frames?
  3. At the bottom, does the working arm hang straight below the shoulder?
  4. At the top, is the elbow above the back line?
  5. At the top, is the dumbbell beside the lower ribs/hip rather than under the armpit?
  6. Does the forearm stay roughly vertical under the elbow in the mid frames?
  7. Do the shoulders stay level (no rotation open) at the top?
  8. Is the dumbbell in the hand in every frame?
- **Sources.** https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/126/single-arm-row/ · https://barbend.com/dumbbell-row/ · https://one.puregym.com/exercises/back/rows/single-arm-dumbbell-row

## A4. Romanian deadlift (barbell)

- **Must-see positions.** (1) Top: standing tall, barbell hanging at arm's length against the upper thighs, knees soft, shoulders over hips. (2) Bottom: hips pushed far back behind the heels, torso pitched forward with a flat back, bar just below the knees and touching or nearly touching the legs, arms vertical, shins near vertical, knees only slightly more bent than at the top.
- **Path.** The hips move backward first and the torso tips as a rigid plank about the hips; the bar slides down the thighs and past the knees in a near-vertical line over the mid-foot. The knees unlock a little early and then hold their angle. At the midpoint the bar is at mid-thigh, the hips are already clearly behind the heels and the knees are no more bent than at the bottom. The return is the reverse: hips drive forward, no separate knee straightening at the end, no lean-back past vertical.
- **Angles that matter.** Knee held at about 15° of flexion by instruction (StrengthLog; Lee 2018 protocol), i.e. included ~165°; measured RDL knee flexion in Lee 2018 was 33.9 ± 12.6° (included ~146°) against 85.2 ± 11.7° for the conventional deadlift — so the visible difference from a deadlift is roughly 50° of knee bend. Will's floor: knee angle ≥ ~130°, torso pitch ~64° (unsourced, from Will's spec). Ankle stays near 90° (Lee: 90.5° RDL vs 111.9° conventional), i.e. shins vertical.
- **Faults.** Knees bending to gain depth (it becomes a squat or conventional deadlift); bar swinging forward away from the legs; spine rounding at the bottom; shins travelling forward; hips dropping straight down instead of back; hyperextending at the top.
- **Readability.** The hip moving backward and the torso tipping carry it; the bar's contact with the legs is the equipment detail. A viewer must be able to tell it from a squat (knees barely bend) and from a floor deadlift (bar stops below the knee, never reaches the floor). Best single camera: pure side-on — every key quantity is in the sagittal plane. The near plate must not cover the knee or the bar-to-leg gap.
- **Constraints.** Distal bicep: double-overhand (pronated) grip, arms dead straight in every frame — any elbow bend loads the bicep. No mixed/underhand grip may be implied. Right peroneal: feet flat, weight mid-foot.
- **Differs from canon.** Depth: canon goes as low as a flat back allows (StrengthLog), and Lee's subjects aimed for the floor; Will's stops just below the knee.
- **Checklist.**
  1. Is the knee angle about 130° or more in every frame?
  2. At the bottom, are the hips clearly behind the heels?
  3. Is the bar within a hand's width of the legs in every frame?
  4. At the bottom, is the bar just below the knees (not at the floor, not above the knee)?
  5. Is the back a straight line from hips to shoulders in every frame?
  6. Are the arms straight and hanging vertically in every frame?
  7. Do the shins stay near vertical throughout?
  8. In the mid frames, has the hip already moved back (not a straight-down drop)?
- **Sources.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6323186 (Lee et al. 2018) · https://www.strengthlog.com/romanian-deadlift/ · https://journals.northumbria.ac.uk/index.php/gjsscmr/article/view/1524

## A5. Face pull (cable rope)

- **Must-see positions.** (1) Start: standing upright facing a cable column, pulley at face height, arms extended toward the pulley at about shoulder/face height, one hand on each rope end, cable taut in a straight line pulley-to-hands. (2) Finish: hands beside the face at about ear/temple level and wider than the head; elbows out to the sides at or above shoulder height; forearms rotated up toward vertical (knuckles toward the ceiling); the two rope ends spread apart in a V from the pulley.
- **Path.** Elbows lead, travelling back and out — never down. The hands follow, separate as they approach the face, and in the last third the forearms rotate upward (external rotation) so the hands finish above or level with the elbows. At the midpoint the upper arms are already abducted with elbows near shoulder height and the forearms roughly horizontal, pointing at the pulley. The cable stays a straight taut line at every instant; torso, hips and feet are still.
- **Angles that matter.** Elbows at or above shoulder height at the finish; forearm–upper arm about 90° at the finish (Breaking Muscle/coaching consensus); pull target forehead height (StrengthLog) — Will's is "toward the eyes". No peer-reviewed external-rotation angle for the face pull was found; the "forearms rotate up" finish is from StrengthLog and coaching sources only.
- **Faults.** Elbows dropping below the shoulders (a row); hands pulled to the chin or chest; hands staying together so the rope never spreads; leaning back or rocking the torso; hands finishing below the elbows (no rotation); slack cable.
- **Readability.** Elbow height relative to the shoulders and the hands arriving beside the face carry it; the cable line and the spreading rope ends are the equipment. A viewer must be able to tell it from a row (elbows high, hands at the face) and see the rope split. Best single camera: three-quarter, roughly 30° forward of side-on — pure side shows elbow height and the cable but hides the hands separating; pure front hides the cable and the pull distance.
- **Constraints.** Distal bicep: overhand (pronated) grip on the rope, which is also what StrengthLog and the NSCA manual specify. AC joint: elbows at shoulder height is the top of the allowed range — the hands must finish beside the face, never behind the head or neck.
- **Differs from canon.** Pulley height: StrengthLog says a "high position" and other guides say just above head height; Will's is face height. Target: canon forehead/nose/ears; Will's "toward the eyes". Both minor.
- **Checklist.**
  1. Is the cable a straight, taut line from pulley to hands in every frame?
  2. At the finish, are the elbows at or above shoulder height?
  3. At the finish, are the hands beside the face and wider apart than the head?
  4. At the finish, are the hands level with or above the elbows (forearms rotated up)?
  5. Do the elbows stay at or above shoulder height in the mid frames (never dip)?
  6. Is the torso upright and unchanged in every frame?
  7. At the start, are the arms fully extended toward the pulley?
- **Sources.** https://www.strengthlog.com/face-pull/ · https://www.nsca.com/education/articles/kinetic-select/face-pull-machine (403 on fetch; cited only for "closed, pronated grip" from the search snippet) · https://breakingmuscle.com/?p=176675

# Workout B

## B1. Trap-bar deadlift

- **Must-see positions.** (1) Bottom/start: standing inside the hexagonal frame, feet about hip width; hands on the two side handles with arms straight down beside the legs; hips back and down, knees well bent, flat back, shoulders above the handles, plates resting on the floor. (2) Lockout: standing fully upright, hips and knees straight, handles at mid-thigh height beside the hips, plates clear of the floor.
- **Path.** Hips and knees extend together and the torso angle opens at the same rate — the hips and shoulders rise in step. The handles (and the plate centres) travel in a vertical line beside the legs, over the mid-foot, never out in front of the body. At the midpoint the handles are about knee height, the knees have partly straightened and the torso has partly risen; the back is the same straight line. Lowering reverses it and the plates touch the floor each rep.
- **Angles that matter.** Bottom: torso pitch ~64°, knee angle ~100–105°, arms ~177° (unsourced, from Will's spec). Rivera 2022 measured a conventional-style hex-bar start at 98.3 ± 6.5° knee angle, rising to 128.4° for a Romanian-style start — so Will's 100–105° is the standard start, about 30–45° more knee bend than his RDL. Swinton 2011 (abstract): the hex bar lowers peak moments at the lumbar spine, hip and ankle and raises the knee moment versus a straight bar — consistent with a more knee-bent, load-beside-the-body lift.
- **Faults.** Hips rising first so the knees straighten while the torso stays down (it turns into an RDL); back rounding; arms bending; the load drawn in front of the shins like a straight bar; plates never reaching the floor; leaning back past vertical at lockout.
- **Readability.** The handles beside the hips (not in front) and the plates meeting the floor carry it. A viewer must be able to tell it from the RDL: knees clearly bent, the load at the sides, and a floor touch. Best single camera: side-on, turned a few degrees toward the front so both arms and the frame beside the body are visible. In side view the near plate overlaps the lower legs — the knee and hip joints must still be visible past it.
- **Constraints.** Distal bicep: neutral handles and dead-straight arms are both required and must both be visible (StrengthLog: straight arms reduce bicep tear risk). AC joint: no issue. Right peroneal: feet flat and still.
- **Differs from canon.** None of substance. Canon offers high or low handles; Will's spec only requires that the plates touch the floor.
- **Checklist.**
  1. Do the plates touch the floor at the bottom and clear it at the top?
  2. Are the hands beside the legs (not in front of the shins) in every frame?
  3. Are the arms straight in every frame?
  4. At the bottom, are the knees clearly bent (about 100–105°), visibly more than the RDL?
  5. Is the back a straight line in every frame?
  6. In the mid frames, have the knees and the torso both partly opened (hips not shooting up first)?
  7. At lockout, are the hips and knees fully straight with the torso vertical?
- **Sources.** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21659894/ (Swinton et al. 2011; abstract only) · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9022700 (Rivera et al. 2022) · https://www.strengthlog.com/trap-bar-deadlift/

## B2. Split squat (bodyweight, static stance)

- **Must-see positions.** (1) Top: long staggered stance, front foot flat, back foot on the ball with the heel up; both knees nearly straight; torso upright; arms hanging. (2) Bottom: front thigh about horizontal with the knee at roughly 90°, front shin near vertical, front heel down; back knee bent to roughly 90°, directly under the hip, hovering just above the floor; torso upright with a slight forward lean.
- **Path.** The pelvis moves straight down and straight up between the feet — no forward or backward travel. Both knees bend together. Neither foot moves; the back foot only pivots over the ball. At the midpoint the hips are half-way down and still centred between the feet, and the front shin is already near vertical. Head and shoulders ride vertically with the hips.
- **Angles that matter.** Front knee ~90° at the bottom (BarBend). Torso lean ~15° (unsourced, from Will's spec; BarBend says "very slight"). Back-knee hover ~7 cm (unsourced, from Will's spec). Weight about 60% front / 40% back (BarBend). Schütz 2014: a more vertical front tibia reduces front-knee range of motion and moves range to the rear knee and hip — so Will's vertical shin is a legitimate execution choice, not the only one.
- **Faults.** Back knee resting on or striking the floor; hips drifting forward so the front knee travels well past the toes and the heel lifts; hips travelling back so the front leg barely works; feet moving (it becomes a lunge); torso folding forward; stance so short that the back knee lands beside the front heel.
- **Readability.** The pelvis's vertical travel and the gap under the back knee carry it. A viewer must be able to tell it from a lunge (no step, both feet fixed) and see the hover gap. Best single camera: pure side-on, low enough that the gap between the back knee and the floor is not closed by perspective. No equipment.
- **Constraints.** Right peroneal: the front foot stays flat with no inward/outward roll; when the right leg is behind, it is on the ball of the foot with the heel straight up, not rolled out. Arms simply hang — nothing to contradict the shoulder or bicep limits.
- **Differs from canon.** (a) BarBend sets the stance so the back knee "reaches the floor"; Will's hovers and never touches. (b) Will lists the front knee passing the toes as a fault; the research treats forward shin angle as a loading choice, not an error. Will's version is the rubric.
- **Checklist.**
  1. Are both feet in the same place in every frame?
  2. At the bottom, is there a visible gap between the back knee and the floor?
  3. At the bottom, is the back knee under the hip?
  4. Is the front shin near vertical in every frame, with the front heel down?
  5. Is the back heel raised in every frame?
  6. Do the hips move vertically (same fore-aft position at top, middle and bottom)?
  7. Is the torso upright or leaning no more than about 15° in every frame?
- **Sources.** https://barbend.com/split-squat-form/ · https://www.sciencegate.app/document/10.1123/jab.2013-0175 (Schütz et al. 2014; abstract only) · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10667687/

## B3. Chest-supported row (incline bench, dumbbells)

- **Must-see positions.** (1) Bottom: chest and belly lying on an incline pad set at roughly 30–45°, head past the top of the pad and in line with the spine; legs extended back with the feet braced on the floor and knees off it; both arms hanging straight down from the shoulders, a dumbbell in each hand just clear of the floor, palms facing each other. (2) Top: both elbows bent and driven back along the ribs so they sit above/behind the plane of the back; dumbbells beside the lower ribs; shoulder blades drawn together; chest still on the pad.
- **Path.** Both arms move together and symmetrically. Because the torso is inclined, the elbows travel up and back toward the hips rather than straight toward the ceiling; the dumbbells rise along the sides of the pad. At the midpoint the elbows are level with the torso and the forearms hang near vertical. A one-beat hold at the top, then a slower lower. Torso, head and legs do not move at all.
- **Angles that matter.** Pad angle 30–45° in most guides (Will's is ~38°); elbows at ~90° at the top (Breaking Muscle); upper arms tucked close to the torso (BarBend). All coaching consensus — no peer-reviewed kinematics found.
- **Faults.** Chest lifting off the pad to heave the weight; shoulders shrugging to the ears; neck craned up; elbows flared out wide to shoulder level; one arm leading the other; dumbbells resting on the floor at the bottom.
- **Readability.** The elbows breaking above the back line and the chest staying on the pad carry it; the incline pad and both dumbbells are the equipment. A viewer must be able to tell the torso is supported by a bench and that both arms row together. Best single camera: side-on, turned slightly to the rear three-quarter so the far arm is not exactly behind the near one and the elbows read against the back.
- **Constraints.** Distal bicep: neutral grip — handles parallel to the torso's long axis in every frame; never palms-forward. AC joint: elbows stay tucked, not flared to shoulder height. Right peroneal: feet only brace.
- **Differs from canon.** None found.
- **Checklist.**
  1. Is the chest in contact with the pad in every frame, with no change in torso position?
  2. At the bottom, do both arms hang straight with the dumbbells off the floor?
  3. At the top, are both elbows above the line of the back?
  4. Do the elbows stay close to the ribs rather than flaring out to the sides?
  5. Are the two arms at the same height in every frame?
  6. Is the head in line with the spine in every frame?
  7. Is a dumbbell in each hand in every frame?
- **Sources.** https://www.coachweb.com/back-exercises/9105/incline-bench-row · https://catalystathletics.com/exercise/824/Dumbbell-Incline-Bench-Row/ · https://www.onepeloton.com/en-GB/blog/chest-supported-row · https://legionathletics.com/chest-supported-row

## B4. Neutral-grip lat pulldown

- **Must-see positions.** (1) Top: seated, thighs horizontal, feet flat; arms fully extended overhead holding a neutral-grip attachment, hands about shoulder width or narrower with palms facing each other; torso leaning back slightly; cable running straight up to an overhead pulley. (2) Bottom: handles at the upper chest/collarbone; elbows pointing down and slightly back, tucked beside the ribs; chest lifted toward the handles; the same lean as at the top.
- **Path.** The elbows drive down and back in an arc beside the body; the handle descends close in front of the face in a straight line along the cable. At the midpoint the handle is about forehead/chin height, the elbows are at about shoulder height and out in front of the torso, not behind it. The cable is taut and straight from pulley to handle at every instant. The torso holds one angle throughout; the return is a controlled full reach, arms straight again.
- **Angles that matter.** Lean-back ~12–15°, fixed (unsourced, from Will's spec; a 10–15° lean is common coaching advice with no primary source found). The 2025 EMG study of seven pulldown variants found no significant difference in latissimus activation between pronated, supinated and neutral grips, or between upright and 30° trunk inclination — so the neutral grip costs nothing.
- **Faults.** Leaning back 30–45° and rowing the handle to the belly; rocking the torso each rep; pulling behind the neck; stopping at chin height (half range); elbows flaring straight out sideways; shoulders shrugged at the bottom; slack or bent cable.
- **Readability.** The handle travelling from full reach to the upper chest and the elbows ending beside the ribs carry it; the overhead cable is the equipment. A viewer must be able to tell the handle ends in front at the chest and the torso does not rock. Best single camera: front three-quarter (about 30–45° off straight-on) — side-on shows the lean and the handle-to-chest but stacks the arms; straight-on hides the lean and the front/behind-the-neck distinction.
- **Constraints.** Distal bicep: palms face each other in every frame; the attachment must never read as an underhand bar. AC joint: narrow hands (no wide grip) and the handle always in front of the head — a frame with the handle behind the neck is an automatic fail.
- **Differs from canon.** Canon lat pulldown is a wide pronated straight bar (and many guides show underhand as a variant). Will's is a close neutral handle; StrengthLog notes the neutral grip is "easier on the shoulders and wrists".
- **Checklist.**
  1. Is the cable a straight, taut line from the overhead pulley to the handle in every frame?
  2. At the top, are the arms fully extended overhead?
  3. At the bottom, is the handle at the upper chest (not chin, not belly)?
  4. Is the handle in front of the head in every frame?
  5. At the bottom, do the elbows point down beside the ribs?
  6. Is the torso lean small (about 12–15°) and identical in every frame?
  7. Are the hands about shoulder width or narrower in every frame?
- **Sources.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12452428/ (2025 EMG study of grip and trunk inclination) · https://www.strengthlog.com/lat-pulldown/ · https://www.researchgate.net/publication/44669065_Grip_Width_and_Forearm_Orientation_Effects_on_Muscle_Activity_During_the_Lat_Pull-Down (Lusk et al. 2010; summary only)

## B5. Band pull-apart

- **Must-see positions.** (1) Start: standing tall, both arms straight out in front at shoulder height, hands about shoulder width apart, palms down, a band held taut between them. (2) Finish: arms straight out to the sides in a T at shoulder height, in line with the shoulders (not swept behind them); the band stretched across and touching the chest; shoulder blades drawn together.
- **Path.** The shoulder blades draw back first, then both arms sweep outward in a horizontal arc at constant height, symmetrically. The band is always a straight line between the two hands, lengthening continuously and moving back toward the chest. At the midpoint each arm is about 45° out from straight ahead, elbows still straight, hands still exactly at shoulder height, band roughly twice its starting length and half-way to the chest. On the return the band shortens but never sags. Torso, head and legs are still.
- **Angles that matter.** Elbows ~177°, effectively straight (unsourced, from Will's spec; Motra allows "slightly bent"). End range: arms in line with the torso or band at the chest (Motra). Fukunaga 2022: a palm-down grip gives the highest upper-trapezius and posterior-deltoid activity, a palm-up grip the highest infraspinatus and lower-trapezius activity.
- **Faults.** Elbows bending so the hands come toward the shoulders (a row); hands dropping below or rising above shoulder height; shoulders shrugging; ribs flaring and the low back arching; arms swinging behind the body; the band drawn slack or curved at any point.
- **Readability.** The band — a single straight line that visibly lengthens several-fold — carries the whole exercise, together with the two straight arms. A viewer must be able to tell the arms stay straight and level. Best single camera: front, turned a little (about 20–30°) and slightly raised so the band's approach to the chest is visible; side-on collapses both the arm sweep and the band into a point and must not be used.
- **Constraints.** Distal bicep: palms down (pronated) or thumbs-up neutral only; the figure must never read as palms-up. AC joint: hands stay at shoulder height and the arms stop in line with the shoulders — no horizontal abduction past the body's plane, nothing behind the neck.
- **Differs from canon.** Several mainstream guides (MasterClass, FitnessVolt) teach an underhand/palms-up grip, and Fukunaga 2022 shows palms-up favours the rotator cuff and lower trapezius. Will's bicep constraint rules that grip out; his version is pronated. Will also requires straight elbows where some guides allow a slight bend.
- **Checklist.**
  1. Is the band a straight line ending exactly at both hands in every frame?
  2. Are both elbows straight in every frame?
  3. Are both hands at shoulder height in every frame?
  4. Does the band get visibly longer from the start frame to the finish frame (several times its start length)?
  5. At the finish, are the arms in a T in line with the shoulders, with the band at the chest?
  6. Are the two arms symmetric in every frame?
  7. Is the torso upright and unchanged in every frame?

## B6. Side plank (forearm)

- **Must-see positions.** (1) The hold (the target): lying on one side, propped on the bottom forearm with the forearm flat on the floor and the elbow directly under the shoulder; legs straight, feet stacked; hips lifted so ankles, knees, hips, shoulders and head form one straight line; top arm resting along the body; body facing squarely sideways, not rolled toward the floor or ceiling. (2) The sag (the demonstrated failure): the same supports, but the pelvis has dropped toward the floor so the line bends downward at the hips.
- **Path.** This is a hold; the demonstration loops between the straight line and the sag. Only the pelvis moves, in a short vertical arc. The planted forearm, the elbow and the feet stay on exactly the same floor spots and the supporting shoulder stays over the elbow, so the body bends at the waist between two fixed ends. At the midpoint the hips are half-way between the sag and the line. The hips never rise above the straight line.
- **Angles that matter.** Elbow directly under the shoulder; upper arm vertical; straight line head to feet (NASM). No numeric sag depth exists in the sources — the amplitude is Will's (unsourced, from Will's spec). McGill's side bridge is the same position; his entry-level version supports from the knees.
- **Faults (in the hold frame).** Elbow ahead of or behind the shoulder; hips piked above the line; torso rolled forward or back; head dropped or craned; feet apart or the top foot on the floor in front. **Faults in the animation:** shoulder or feet sliding during the sag; the whole body lowering as a rigid plank instead of bending at the hips; the sag frame reading as the target because it is held longer or looks more settled than the line.
- **Readability.** The line from ankles through hips to shoulders, and the forearm on the floor under the shoulder, carry it. A viewer must be able to tell which frame is the goal (straight) and which is the fault (sag), and that the support is the forearm, not a straight arm. Best single camera: facing the front of the body, perpendicular to its long axis and low to the floor, so the body line is drawn at full length and the hip height reads directly; an end-on or steep overhead view foreshortens the line to nothing.
- **Constraints.** AC joint: forearm support, never a straight-arm side plank; the elbow under the shoulder keeps the joint stacked. Right peroneal: with stacked feet the outer edge of the bottom foot bears the load — on the right-side hold that is the right foot's outer border, so the feet must look stacked and still, not rolling (unsourced observation; Will's spec keeps feet stacked).
- **Differs from canon.** Canon is a motionless hold. Will's demonstration deliberately animates the sag-and-recover so the failure mode is visible; the rubric accepts the sag as intended and tests that the recovered frame is a true straight line.
- **Checklist.**
  1. Is the forearm flat on the floor with the elbow under the shoulder in every frame?
  2. Do the elbow and the feet stay on the same floor spots in every frame?
  3. In the highest frame, are ankles, hips, shoulders and head on one straight line?
  4. Do the hips never rise above that line in any frame?
  5. In the sag frames, does only the pelvis drop while the shoulder stays over the elbow?
  6. Are the feet stacked in every frame?
  7. Is the top arm along the body in every frame?
