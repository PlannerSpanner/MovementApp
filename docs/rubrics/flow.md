# Morning Flow — acceptance rubrics (13 movements)

**Method.** Each rubric is written to Will's spec in `docs/movement-reference.md` (Morning Flow + user constraints + sides protocol), then checked against outside form sources found by web search on 2026-11-12. Where the two differ, Will's version is the rubric and a "Differs from canon" line says how.
**Source quality.** Strong (clinical / peer-reviewed / certifying body) for Cobra, Cat-cows, Thread the needle, 90/90, knee-drive height and thoracic rotation range. Medium (PT-run exercise libraries, yoga teaching sites) for hip CARs, down dog, side bend, serratus punch. Weak or none for Bouncing, Arm sweeps, Chest opener swings and the Will-specific windmill sequence: these are personal qi-gong-style variants and are marked "(unsourced, from Will's spec)".
**How sources were read.** `[F]` = page fetched and read; `[S]` = seen only as a search-result summary (page blocked or not opened), so treat wording as approximate. Yoga Journal, Physiopedia and YMAA pages blocked direct fetch.
**Numbers.** Angles are human joint angles, not engine parameters. Ranges quoted from population norms are ceilings for plausibility, not targets.
**Checklists** assume eight stills at phases 0, 0.125 ... 0.875 of one cycle. "Every frame" means all eight; "some frame" means at least one. For side-switching movements, run the checklist once per side.

## 1. Bouncing in place

- **Must-see positions**
  1. *Airborne*: body vertical, legs nearly straight, ankles pointed so the toes hang below the heels, both feet clear of the floor by a small gap (an inch or two, about a toe's thickness on screen).
  2. *Landed tall*: balls of the feet on the floor first, then heels; knees soft, not locked.
  3. *Soft dip*: feet flat, knees bent a little (roughly 20-30 degrees, unsourced), hips slightly lower, trunk still vertical.
- **Path**: ankles drive it. Dip, ankles extend, lift-off, toe-first landing, dip. Head, trunk and pelvis travel straight up and down as one loose column; arms hang and lag a fraction behind (a slight jiggle). Midpoint between airborne and dip is toes-down, heels-up contact, not a flat-footed slap. Feet do not move sideways or fore-aft.
- **Angles that matter**: no published numbers for this drill. Flight height is the acceptance number: low enough to read as a bounce, not a jump (unsourced, from Will's spec).
- **Faults**: feet never leaving the floor (reads as a knee pulse); a jump with visible hang time or deep squat landing; flat-foot landing; locked knees; shoulders raised toward the ears; feet drifting; arms held rigid or pumping.
- **Readability**: the feet and the gap under them carry the movement, so the floor line and both feet must be visible in every frame. Best view: side-on or 3/4 front, where toe-down versus flat foot and the knee dip are both visible. Straight front view hides the knee bend.
- **Constraints**: right peroneal tendon. Low flight, toe-first soft landing, no lateral foot motion, no ankle roll. Fallback (not what the animation shows) is heel-to-toe rocking.
- **Differs from canon**: the qi-gong loosening bounce found in outside sources keeps the feet on the ground (YMAA: bounce "without letting the feet leave the ground") [S]. Will's version deliberately leaves the floor (changed 2026-10-12). His spec wins.
- **Checklist**
  1. Is there some frame with both feet clearly off the floor, toes lower than heels?
  2. Is the gap under the feet small in every airborne frame (no jump)?
  3. Is there some frame with feet flat and knees visibly bent?
  4. Is the trunk vertical in every frame?
  5. Are the feet in the same horizontal place in every frame?
  6. Do the arms hang at the sides, shoulders low, in every frame?
- **Sources**: https://ymaa.com/articles/basic-warm-up-and-stretching-exercises [S]; https://commongroundmeditation.org/qigong-movements [S]. No reputable step-by-step source for the airborne variant.

## 2. Arm sweeps with knee dip

- **Must-see positions**
  1. *Top (extension arc)*: feet flat, legs near straight, hips pushed forward of the ankles, chest lifted, both arms overhead and slightly behind the head, head back. Seen from the side the body is one shallow backward bow from heels to fingertips.
  2. *Midpoint (hinge)*: flat back near 45 degrees to horizontal or lower, knees starting to bend, arms roughly horizontal reaching forward of the shoulders on the way down (and again on the way up).
  3. *Bottom (fold)*: trunk folded over the thighs, head hanging low toward the floor, knees bent, both arms swung through past the shins so the hands are behind the lower legs.
- **Path**: both arms move together as one pendulum in the sagittal plane, phase-locked with the knees: knees straightest at the top, most bent at the bottom. Hips travel back as the trunk folds (a hinge), forward as it rises. Heels stay down throughout. No pause at either end.
- **Angles that matter**: standing lumbar extension is small (about 15 degrees on Physiopedia's table [S]), so the top arc must come from hips-forward plus thoracic lift plus arms behind the ears, not a sharp low-back crease. In the fold the hips should stay higher than the knees (hinge, not squat) (unsourced, from Will's spec).
- **Faults**: half-range swing (arms stop at shoulder height or hands never pass the shins); arms and knees out of phase; squat (hips dropping to knee height, trunk upright); heels lifting; a kink at the waist instead of a smooth arc at the top; arms splitting apart.
- **Readability**: the arms and the trunk line carry it. Pure side view is best: the pendulum, the back arc and the hands passing behind the shins all lie in that plane. From the front the whole movement foreshortens to nothing.
- **Constraints**: right AC joint. Overhead-and-back is momentum-led and must not look wrenched to end range; arms stay shoulder-width, not wide. Mid-back T4-T8: the top arc is welcome thoracic extension.
- **Differs from canon**: no canonical exercise. Closest outside descriptions are qi-gong "pendulum arm swings" with a gentle knee bend and no fold [S]. Will's large fold-and-arc version is his own.
- **Checklist**
  1. Is there a frame with the arms overhead and behind the head line and the hips forward of the heels?
  2. Is there a frame with the head below hip height and the hands behind the shins?
  3. Are the knees most bent in the same frame the trunk is most folded?
  4. In the frames between, is the back straight and the arms near horizontal?
  5. Are both heels on the floor in every frame?
  6. Are the hips above knee height in every frame?
  7. Do the two arms stay together in every frame?
- **Sources**: https://mpgtaijiquan.blogspot.com/2021/06/swinging-arms-qigong-exercises.html [S]; https://www.physio-pedia.com/Lumbar_Spine_Range_Of_Motion [S]. Sequence itself: (unsourced, from Will's spec).

## 3. Punching rotations

- **Must-see positions**
  1. *Stance (held throughout)*: feet well outside shoulder width, toes slightly out, knees bent and tracking over the toes, pelvis level and facing forward, trunk upright.
  2. *Punch end, one side*: the punching arm straight at shoulder height, fist across the body's midline; that shoulder visibly forward of the other (shoulder line turned roughly 45-50 degrees to the hips); shoulder blade pushed forward so the arm looks longer than its resting length; other fist drawn back at the ribs, elbow behind the trunk.
  3. *Punch end, other side*: mirror.
- **Path**: the turn starts at the waist; the shoulder line rotates, the punching arm extends as the other retracts, and the last part of the reach is the shoulder blade sliding forward. Midpoint: shoulders square to the hips, both elbows bent with fists near the ribs or chest. Head height constant. Slow, not snapped.
- **Angles that matter**: punch at shoulder height (arm about 90 degrees of shoulder flexion). Trunk rotation in standing with a free pelvis averages 40-60 degrees per side in healthy adults (Johnson et al. 2012, seated/half-kneeling measures) [F], so a roughly 50 degree shoulder-line swing is plausible without the hips turning much. Horse-stance sources agree on wide, low, torso upright; they disagree on exact width [S].
- **Faults**: stance bobbing up and down; knees caving inward; punch above or below shoulder height; shoulder hiked toward the ear instead of pushed forward; no trunk rotation (arm-only punch); pelvis swinging with the shoulders; non-punching arm dangling.
- **Readability**: the punching fist and the shoulder line carry it. The punch must travel across the screen, not toward the lens, on both sides, so view from directly in front of the person. Left and right arms must be distinguishable when they cross.
- **Constraints**: right AC joint. Slow, shoulder height, no ballistic lock-out at the end. Mid-back: the rotation is the therapeutic part.
- **Differs from canon**: the clinical serratus punch isolates the shoulder blade and says the body should not rotate (Physitrack) [S]. Will's version adds a waist rotation and a horse stance. On-screen it animates the leading side and swaps at the halfway chime.
- **Checklist**
  1. Are the feet in the same place and the hips at the same height in every frame?
  2. Is there a frame with one arm straight at shoulder height and its fist past the midline?
  3. In that frame is the other fist at the ribs?
  4. Is the punching shoulder clearly ahead of the other in that frame?
  5. Are the knees over the toes (not collapsed in) in every frame?
  6. Is the trunk upright in every frame?
- **Sources**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26749459/ (serratus punch EMG) [S]; https://us.physitrack.com/home-exercise-video/bilateral-arm-serratus-anterior-punch [S]; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3418115 [F]; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_stance [S].

## 4. Dynamic lateral reaches

- **Must-see positions**
  1. *Reach to one side*: feet wide and flat, pelvis level and centred between the feet, trunk bent sideways in one smooth C-curve, the far-side arm overhead reaching past the head in the direction of the lean, the near-side hand on the hip.
  2. *Upright crossover*: trunk vertical, arms trading roles; the outgoing arm lowering through a sideways T, the incoming arm rising out to the side.
  3. *Reach to the other side*: mirror.
- **Path**: everything stays in the frontal plane. The reaching arm travels in a sideways arc (hip, out to the side, overhead, past the head) and returns the same way. One side per slow breath. Pelvis and feet stay still; the head follows the trunk line.
- **Angles that matter**: lumbar lateral flexion is about 20 degrees per side on Physiopedia's table [S]; with thoracic contribution a trunk line 20-30 degrees off vertical is realistic. More than about 40 degrees means the hips have slid out.
- **Faults**: pelvis shifting sideways while the trunk stays straight (a lean, not a bend); far-side heel or foot lifting; trunk tipping forward or twisting; reaching arm in front of the face instead of over the head; both hands on hips or both overhead; rushing.
- **Readability**: the trunk curve and reaching arm carry it and lie in the frontal plane, so view from directly in front. Any oblique view turns the side-bend into an apparent fore-aft rock. The hip-hand must be visibly on the hip, and it must be clear which arm is up.
- **Constraints**: right-side lateral-line asymmetry is the target; both sides should reach the same depth. Peroneal: both feet stay flat, no rolling to the outer edge of the far foot. AC joint: the overhead arm is a reach, not a yank.
- **Differs from canon**: Cedars-Sinai's side-bend allows a slight forward lean and a hip-width stance [F]; Will's is a wide stance, strictly frontal plane, one hand on the hip, continuous side to side.
- **Checklist**
  1. Are both feet flat and in the same place in every frame?
  2. Is the pelvis level and centred in every frame?
  3. Is there a frame on each side with the trunk curved sideways and the opposite arm past the head?
  4. Is the non-reaching hand on the hip in those frames?
  5. Is the trunk vertical with arms in transition in the frame(s) between?
  6. Does the head stay in the plane of the body (no forward duck) in every frame?
- **Sources**: https://www.cedars-sinai.org/health-topics/the-side-bend-stretch-a-gentle-exercise [F]; https://us.physitrack.com/home-exercise-video/standing-side-stretch [S]; https://www.physio-pedia.com/Lumbar_Spine_Range_Of_Motion [S].

## 5. Cross-body knee drives

- **Must-see positions**
  1. *Tall start*: both feet under the hips, trunk vertical, arms bent.
  2. *Top of the drive*: standing leg straight; the driving thigh at or above horizontal with the knee pulled across toward the midline; shin hanging roughly vertical under the knee; the opposite elbow brought down and across toward that knee; shoulders rotated toward the lifted knee; chest still high.
  3. *Return*: foot back beside the other, arms reset.
- **Path**: knee rises and crosses as the opposite elbow descends and crosses; they approach each other in front of the navel-to-chest zone. The meeting comes from the knee rising and the trunk rotating, not the chest dropping. The other arm swings back as counterbalance. Midpoint: thigh about 45 degrees up, trunk beginning to turn.
- **Angles that matter**: thigh parallel to the ground is the standard high-knee target (NSCA high-knee drill; others say "at least parallel") [S], i.e. about 90 degrees of hip flexion, which matches Will's "at least hip-crease height". Elbow-knee contact is not required; a forced touch by rounding is a fault [S].
- **Faults**: trunk rounding or bowing forward to reach the knee; knee below hip height; knee straight up with no cross; same-side elbow to knee; standing knee bent or caving inward; leaning back.
- **Readability**: the lifted knee and the opposite elbow carry it; the viewer must tell that it is the opposite elbow. Best view: front or 3/4 front, where the across-the-body component is visible. Pure side view hides the cross.
- **Constraints**: peroneal. No hop on the standing foot; the driving foot is placed back down, not stamped. AC joint: elbows stay bent and in front, nothing overhead.
- **Differs from canon**: none of substance. As performed it alternates each rep; the animation shows one leading side and swaps at the halfway chime (Will's sides protocol).
- **Checklist**
  1. Is there a frame with the driving thigh at or above horizontal?
  2. In that frame is the knee inside the line of its own hip (crossing toward the midline)?
  3. In that frame is the opposite elbow, not the same-side elbow, nearest the knee?
  4. Is the standing leg straight and its foot planted in every frame?
  5. Is the head at about the same height in every frame (no crunch down)?
  6. Is there a frame with both feet on the floor?
- **Sources**: https://www.nsca.com/education/articles/kinetic-select/high-knee-drills/ [S]; https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/running-form-drills [S]; https://fitwill.app/exercise/7756/standing-crossover-crunch/ (low-grade, cross-body cueing only) [S].

## 6. Chest opener swings

- **Must-see positions**
  1. *Open*: trunk upright, both arms out to the sides at shoulder height and swept behind the line of the shoulders (hands about 25 degrees behind the frontal plane), elbows nearly straight, chest forward, shoulder blades together.
  2. *Pass-through*: arms straight out to the sides in a T, then straight ahead, still at shoulder height.
  3. *Cross*: arms crossed in front of the chest at shoulder height, forearms overlapping, upper back slightly broadened.
  4. *Next cross*: same, with the other arm on top.
- **Path**: both arms swing in a horizontal plane at shoulder height, symmetric and simultaneous. Trunk, head, pelvis and feet stay still. Rhythm is a swing with a soft turnaround at each end, not a held stretch and not a fling.
- **Angles that matter**: shoulder horizontal abduction past the frontal plane is usually quoted at 30-45 degrees (Physiopedia goniometry 45 expected; fitness sources 30-40) [S]. Will's 25 degrees behind sits inside that and short of end range. Horizontal adduction to about 130-140 degrees [S] comfortably permits the forearm cross.
- **Faults**: open phase stopping at the T (arms never get behind the shoulder line); arms sagging toward waist height; hands flung far behind with the chest thrown forward and back arched; trunk rocking; same arm on top every time; shoulders shrugging.
- **Readability**: both arms carry it. The behind-the-plane position is the hard part to show: a straight front view cannot show it, and a pure side view stacks both arms on top of each other. A 3/4 front view from slightly above shows the open sweep; whichever view is used, which arm is on top in the cross must be legible.
- **Constraints**: right AC joint is the governing constraint. Cross-body adduction and end-range horizontal abduction both load it, so both ends must look easy and short of end range.
- **Differs from canon**: generic "hug swings / shoulder crossovers" appear in warm-up lists (ACSM blog names shoulder crossovers) [S] without specifying how far back the arms go. The behind-the-frontal-plane requirement and the deliberate end-range cap are Will's.
- **Checklist**
  1. Are both hands at shoulder height (within a hand's width) in every frame?
  2. Is there a frame where both hands are behind the shoulder line?
  3. Is there a frame where the forearms cross in front of the chest?
  4. Is the trunk upright and still in every frame?
  5. Are the feet planted in every frame?
  6. Across two consecutive cycles, does the top arm in the cross alternate?
- **Sources**: https://www.physio-pedia.com/Goniometry:_Shoulder_Horizontal_Abduction [S]; https://www.physiotutors.com/wiki/shoulder-passive-range-of-motion/ [S]; https://acsm.org/blog-detail/acsm-certified-blog/2021/09/13/developing-warmup-resistance-exercise-dynamic-flexibility-exercises [S]. Alternating-top and 25 degree figures: (unsourced, from Will's spec).

## 7. Single-leg hip circles (standing hip CAR)

- **Must-see positions** (first half: back-to-front)
  1. *Both feet down*: standing tall, hands on hips.
  2. *Behind*: one thigh extended behind the body, knee bent about 90 degrees so the heel is up toward the glute; trunk still vertical.
  3. *Out to the side*: thigh lifted out sideways, knee bent, shin hanging or trailing.
  4. *Front*: thigh horizontal in front, knee at hip height, shin hanging down.
  5. *Down*: foot lands beside the other foot. Then the other leg repeats.
- **Path**: the knee traces one large continuous circle around the hip: behind, side, front, down. The knee stays bent near 90 degrees the whole way. Pelvis stays level and facing forward; trunk does not lean; the standing leg stays straight. After the halfway chime the same circle runs front-to-back. Midpoints matter: between behind and side the knee is out-and-back at mid height, not dropped to the floor.
- **Angles that matter**: adult hip norms (AAOS-technique table in Sports Health 2020 [S]): flexion about 128, extension about 17, abduction about 33-45, internal and external rotation about 42-44 degrees each. So: front station thigh horizontal (90 degrees flexion, well within range); side station thigh no more than about 45 degrees out from vertical with a level pelvis; behind station thigh only about 15-20 degrees past vertical. A thigh shown horizontal to the side or far behind with a level pelvis is anatomically false.
- **Faults**: trunk leaning away from the lifted leg; pelvis hiking or turning with the leg; standing knee bending or caving in; circle collapsed to a front-back swing (no out-to-the-side station); leg straightening; foot not returning to the floor between legs; same leg twice in a row.
- **Readability**: the lifted knee's path carries it. The viewer must see the circle as a circle and tell which leg is up. 3/4 front view is best: a pure front view flattens the front and behind stations, a pure side view flattens the side station.
- **Constraints**: right peroneal tendon. The standing ankle is on single-leg balance, so the standing foot must look quiet and flat (no rolling to the outer edge), and the landing is a placed foot, not a drop.
- **Differs from canon**: FRC-style hip CARs go flexion first (maximal, knee toward chest), then abduction, internal rotation, extension, adduction, then reverse, with whole-body bracing and usually several reps on one leg holding a support (Prehab Guys [F]; other FRC-derived guides [S]). Will's version starts back-to-front, caps the front station at hip height, keeps the knee near 90 degrees, touches down after every circle, alternates legs each circle, and keeps hands on hips.
- **Checklist**
  1. Is the standing leg straight and vertical in every frame where a foot is off the floor?
  2. Are the shoulders level and the trunk vertical in every frame?
  3. Is there a frame with the lifted knee at hip height in front?
  4. Is there a frame with the lifted knee clearly out to the side?
  5. Is there a frame with the thigh behind the body and the heel raised?
  6. Do both feet touch the floor at some frame, and does the other leg lift afterwards?
  7. Is the lifted knee bent in every airborne frame?
  8. Are the hands on the hips in every frame?
- **Sources**: https://theprehabguys.com/vimeo-video/standing-hip-car/ [F]; https://theprehabguys.com/intro-to-frc [S]; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8167346 (hip ROM norms) [S].

## 8. Bent rotation sweeps (windmill, both hands touch down)

- **Must-see positions**
  1. *Both hands down (crossover)*: feet wide, hips hinged back, trunk near horizontal or lower, both knees soft, both hands on the floor between or just ahead of the feet, head down, upper back allowed to round.
  2. *Open to one side*: one hand still on the floor; chest rolled open so the other arm points straight up; the two arms form one near-vertical line through the shoulders; head turned to look sideways or up; the leg on the raised-arm side straight, the leg on the planted-hand side with a softened knee and the hips shifted over it.
  3. *Both hands down again.*
  4. *Open to the other side*: mirror, with the other hand planted.
- **Path**: from both-hands-down, one hand stays; the free arm sweeps out and up as the rib cage rolls. Return the same way until both hands are on the floor, then open to the other side. The pelvis keeps facing the floor and stays at roughly the same height; the trunk never comes up toward standing. Midpoint of the opening: free arm horizontal, pointing out to the side, chest half-turned.
- **Angles that matter**: thoracic rotation with the lumbar spine locked averages about 41 degrees per side, SD about 11 (Johnson et al. 2012) [F]. A vertical arm line therefore needs the top shoulder to open (horizontal abduction, 30-45 degrees available [S]) and a small pelvic contribution, which the asymmetric knees provide. The acceptance test is the arm line vertical, not the chest turned a full 90 degrees.
- **Faults**: standing up between sides; skipping the both-hands-down position (arms staying opposite like a propeller); planted hand floating above the floor; pelvis spinning to face sideways; both legs locked straight or both deeply bent; top arm stopping at horizontal; hands hidden behind the legs.
- **Readability**: the two arms and the roll of the shoulder line carry it. The viewer must tell which hand is planted and that both hands meet the floor between sides. View from in front of the person (they hinge toward the viewer) or 3/4 front; from the side the vertical arm stack survives but left and right openings look identical and the planted hand hides behind the legs.
- **Constraints**: mid-back T4-T8 is the target. Right AC joint: the top arm reaches the vertical by the chest rolling, not by being levered behind the shoulder; nothing bounces at the top. Peroneal: both feet stay flat while weight shifts toward the bent knee, no rolling onto the outer edge.
- **Differs from canon**: the common windmill toe touch reaches each hand to the opposite foot with legs straight and no shared touch-down [S]. Will's version plants the hand on the floor, requires both hands down between rotations, and uses the straight-leg/soft-knee weight shift (sequence per Will, 2026-10-15).
- **Checklist**
  1. Is there a frame with both hands on the floor?
  2. Is there a frame on each side with one hand on the floor and the other arm pointing straight up?
  3. Is the planted hand different on the two sides?
  4. In each open frame is the leg under the raised arm the straighter one?
  5. Are the hips at roughly the same height in every frame (no standing up)?
  6. Are both feet in the same place and flat in every frame?
  7. Are both hands visible (not hidden behind the legs) in the both-hands-down frame?
- **Sources**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3418115 [F]; https://resources.specialolympics.org/sports-essentials/sports-and-coaching/warm-up-and-cool-down-videos/dynamic-warm-up-windmill-toe-touches [S]. Planted-hand sequence and leg action: (unsourced, from Will's spec).

## 9. Cat-cows (quadruped)

- **Must-see positions**
  1. *Neutral tabletop*: hands under the shoulders, knees under the hips, arms and thighs vertical, back flat, neck in line.
  2. *Cat*: whole spine rounded toward the ceiling, highest point around the mid-back, tailbone tucked under, head dropped with the chin toward the chest.
  3. *Cow*: belly dropped toward the floor, back sagging into an arch, chest pushed forward and open between the arms, shoulder blades drawn together, tailbone lifted, head up with eyes forward.
- **Path**: hands, knees and shins never move. Arms and thighs stay vertical while the spine between them changes shape, passing through flat on every half cycle. Head and pelvis move with the spine: both down in cat, both up in cow. The curve should be spread along the whole spine, not a single hinge.
- **Angles that matter**: no reliable published angle for the pose. Visually, the cat peak rises a few inches above the neutral back line and the cow trough drops a similar amount. Will's emphasis is thoracic (T4-T8) extension in cow: the sag should be visible between the shoulder blades, not only at the waist.
- **Faults**: hips rocking back toward the heels or shoulders drifting ahead of the hands; elbows bending to fake the cow; movement only at the low back with a rigid upper back; head moving opposite to the spine; back staying straight while only the head nods.
- **Readability**: the back's outline carries it. Pure side view, with the far arm and far leg hidden behind the near ones, gives the cleanest silhouette. Head direction (down vs forward) must be legible.
- **Constraints**: mid-back T4-T8 is the target, so the upper back must visibly take part in both directions. AC joint: arms vertical and load symmetric; no sinking into the shoulders in cow.
- **Differs from canon**: ACE holds each end 10-15 seconds and sets the feet dorsiflexed (toes tucked) [F]; Will's is a continuous breath-paced flow with feet trailing. On elbows, a PT library says keep them straight [S]; Will's fault list says elbows should not "lock the movement out". Rubric position: arms stay straight and vertical, shoulders free to move.
- **Checklist**
  1. Are both hands and both knees in the same place in every frame?
  2. Are the arms and the thighs vertical in every frame?
  3. Is there a frame where the back is convex toward the ceiling and the head hangs down?
  4. Is there a frame where the back is concave and the head is lifted, face forward?
  5. Is the back flat in the frames between?
  6. Is the curve visible in the upper back, not only at the waist?
- **Sources**: https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/15/cat-cow/ [F]; https://www.columbiadoctors.org/health-library/multimedia/cat-cow/ [S]; https://library.theprehabguys.com/vimeo-video/cat-cow-thoracic-spine-bias/ [S].

## 10. Cobra press-ups (McKenzie prone press-up)

- **Must-see positions**
  1. *Down*: face down, body flat, legs long and relaxed, hands on the floor under or just beside the shoulders, elbows bent and pointing back along the ribs.
  2. *Up*: chest and shoulders lifted by the arms, elbows straighter but still close to the body, pelvis and thighs on the floor, back in one smooth backward arch from pelvis to head, head in line or looking forward, shoulders away from the ears.
- **Path**: the arms push; the back and legs stay passive. The chest travels up and slightly forward in an arc about the pelvis. Hips, thighs, knees and feet do not move. Lower under control along the same path. Midpoint: forearms angled, chest a hand's height off the floor, arch already visible in the low back.
- **Angles that matter**: an MRI study of the press-up measured about 3.4-4.7 degrees of added extension at each lumbar level from L1-2 to L5-S1, roughly 21 degrees in total over resting prone (Kim et al., BMC Musculoskelet Disord 2007) [F]; fluoroscopy shows the press-up gives more lumbosacral lordosis than prone lying or prone-on-elbows (Bo and Park 2012) [S]. So the arch is real but moderate, and it should be concentrated low with a long upper back; a trunk line from pelvis to shoulders of roughly 30-45 degrees above the floor at the top is plausible.
- **Faults**: pelvis or thighs lifting (becomes up-dog); elbows flaring out sideways; shoulders shrugged to the ears; head thrown back; knees bending or feet lifting; a straight plank-like trunk with no arch; hands far in front of the shoulders.
- **Readability**: the trunk outline and pelvis-on-floor contact carry it. Pure side view: shows the arch, the grounded pelvis and the elbow staying near the ribs in one silhouette.
- **Constraints**: right AC joint governs. Elbows tucked close to the ribs throughout; the top is never forced, so arms need not lock. T4-T8: extension spreading into the mid-back is welcome.
- **Differs from canon**: McKenzie extension-in-lying has the patient straighten the arms and push toward end range, pelvis sagging, typically 10 reps (Physiopedia; StatPearls) [S]. Will's version stops short of forced end range and specifies tucked elbows. Pelvis-down is common to both.
- **Checklist**
  1. Is the pelvis on the floor in every frame?
  2. Are the thighs, knees and feet on the floor and unmoved in every frame?
  3. Is there a frame with the chest on or near the floor?
  4. Is there a frame with the chest lifted and the back visibly arched?
  5. Are the elbows beside the ribs (not out to the sides) in every frame?
  6. Are the hands in the same place, under the shoulders, in every frame?
  7. Is there space between ears and shoulders in the top frame?
- **Sources**: https://www.physio-pedia.com/McKenzie_Method [S]; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK539720/ [S]; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1794409 [F]; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jpts/24/3/24_271/_article/-char/en [S].

## 11. Pedaling down dog

- **Must-see positions**
  1. *Inverted V*: hands and feet on the floor, hips the highest point, arms and trunk in one straight line from wrists to hips, head between the upper arms, legs sloping back to the feet.
  2. *Pedal one side*: one leg straight with its heel pressed down toward the floor; the other knee bent forward with that heel lifted and weight on the ball of the foot; the pelvis tips slightly toward the bent-knee side.
  3. *Pedal other side*: mirror.
- **Path**: only the legs alternate. Hands stay planted, the arm-trunk line stays long, and the hips stay high and roughly central. Midpoint: both knees slightly bent, both heels a little off the floor. Feet stay pointing straight ahead and do not walk.
- **Angles that matter**: an angle at the hips of roughly 80-100 degrees between trunk and legs reads as a proper V (unsourced visual convention). Heels do not have to reach the floor (Yoga International) [F]; the straight-side heel should be clearly lower than the bent-side heel. Bent-side knee flexion about 30-45 degrees (unsourced).
- **Faults**: hips dropping toward a plank; rounded upper back or bent elbows; shoulders sagging toward the floor; both knees bending together (a bounce); feet turning out or rolling to the outer edge; heel bouncing rapidly; feet stepping.
- **Readability**: the two legs carry it and must be told apart: which knee is bent and which heel is down. Pure side view hides the far leg behind the near one unless the limbs are clearly differentiated; a 3/4 rear or 3/4 side view separates the legs while keeping the V. The V itself reads best side-on.
- **Constraints**: right peroneal tendon governs. Feet track straight ahead, no outward roll, slow heel press with no bounce. AC joint: arms push the floor away with shoulders stable, not sunk.
- **Differs from canon**: none of substance. Pedaling is a standard warm-up variation (Queensland Health sequence: bend one knee and rise on those toes while pushing the other heel down) [S].
- **Checklist**
  1. Are the hips the highest point of the body in every frame?
  2. Are both hands in the same place in every frame?
  3. Is the line from wrists through shoulders to hips straight in every frame?
  4. Is there a frame with the left knee bent and the right leg straight, and another with the reverse?
  5. In those frames is the straight-leg heel lower than the bent-leg heel?
  6. Do the feet point straight ahead and stay in place in every frame?
- **Sources**: https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/18/downward-facing-dog/ [S]; https://yogainternational.com/article/view/7-common-misconceptions-about-down-dog/ [F]; https://hw.qld.gov.au/?p=41320 [S]; https://www.yogajournal.com/poses/downward-facing-dog/ (login wall, not read).

## 12. Thread the needle

- **Must-see positions**
  1. *Tabletop*: hands under shoulders, knees under hips.
  2. *Reach*: one hand stays planted with that arm vertical; the chest rolls open toward the other side; the free arm points straight up at the ceiling in line with the planted arm; head turned to follow the raised hand; hips still over the knees.
  3. *Thread*: the free arm passes under the chest, palm up, behind the planted hand and out the far side along the floor; that shoulder lowers toward the floor and the side of the head follows; the planted arm bends at the elbow to allow it; hips still over the knees.
- **Path**: one continuous roll of the rib cage about the spine's long axis. From the reach the arm comes down in an arc, the elbow folds as the hand passes under the chest, then the arm lengthens along the floor on the far side. Midpoint is roughly tabletop with the free hand just off the floor beneath its shoulder. Knees, shins and the planted hand never move.
- **Angles that matter**: thoracic rotation in a lumbar-locked quadruped test averages about 41 degrees per side, SD about 11 (Johnson et al. 2012) [F]. The arm reaching vertical therefore combines about 40 degrees of trunk roll with shoulder opening; a chest shown turned fully side-on (90 degrees) with square hips is beyond normal range. In the thread, the shoulder lowering toward the floor comes mainly from the planted elbow bending and the shoulder blade sliding forward.
- **Faults**: hips sitting back toward the heels or swinging out sideways; the pelvis rolling with the chest; weight dumped onto the head or neck; threading arm passing in front of the planted hand or staying straight and stiff; raised arm stopping at horizontal; planted hand shifting.
- **Readability**: the moving arm and the roll of the shoulder line carry it. The viewer must tell planted arm from threading arm at every instant, including when they cross. View from the head end at 3/4, or from the side the chest opens toward: a pure side view hides the threaded arm behind the planted one.
- **Constraints**: mid-back T4-T8 and right-shoulder internal-rotation limit are the targets. Right AC joint: the top reach is unloaded and not levered back past vertical; the bottom rests shoulder toward the floor without weight crushing onto the joint.
- **Differs from canon**: ACE's version rocks the hips back toward the heels to lock the lumbar spine and threads first, reaching second [F]. Hinge Health and Physitrack keep hips over knees, as Will does [F][S]. Will's order is reach first, then thread, hips over knees. Resting head and shoulder on the floor is described by Hinge Health as the deeper option [F].
- **Checklist**
  1. Are both knees and the planted hand in the same place in every frame?
  2. Are the hips directly over the knees in every frame?
  3. Is there a frame with the free arm pointing straight up?
  4. Is there a frame with the free arm under the chest and its hand beyond the planted hand?
  5. In that frame is the threading shoulder lower than the other shoulder?
  6. Does the head turn with the chest (up in the reach, down and sideways in the thread)?
  7. Can the two arms be told apart in every frame?
- **Sources**: https://www.acefitness.org/education-and-resources/professional/expert-articles/7960/thoracic-mobility-exercises-to-address-shoulder-pain [F]; https://www.hingehealth.com/gb/en/resources/articles/thread-the-needle/ [F]; https://au.physitrack.com/home-exercise-video/quadruped-thread-the-needle [S]; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3418115 [F].

## 13. 90/90 with a fold

- **Must-see positions**
  1. *Tall Z-sit*: seated on the floor. Front leg: thigh pointing forward from the hip, knee bent 90 degrees, shin lying across in front of the body parallel to the chest, outer thigh and outer shin on the floor. Back leg: thigh pointing out to the side, knee bent 90 degrees, shin pointing straight back, inner thigh and inner shin on the floor. Trunk upright, shoulders level and square to the front shin.
  2. *Fold*: trunk tipped forward from the hips over the front shin with a straight back, chest moving toward the shin, head in line with the spine; hands may come to the floor either side of the front leg. Legs unchanged.
- **Path**: only the trunk moves, rotating forward and back about the hip joints as one rigid, long line. Both legs and the pelvis contact stay put. Midpoint: trunk about 20-30 degrees forward, still straight. Slow, no bounce at the bottom.
- **Angles that matter**: three right angles define the shape: front knee 90, back knee 90, and about 90 between the two thighs (Cleveland Clinic: front calf parallel to the torso, back lower leg pointing straight back, torso "a nice vertical rectangle") [F]. Front hip in external rotation, back hip in internal rotation; normal ranges are about 42-44 degrees each [S], which is why many people cannot sit fully level, and a slight lean toward the front hip is realistic. The fold is "as if taking a bow", back straight, no spinal rounding [F]; forward fold depth of 30-45 degrees from vertical with a straight back is a realistic full expression (unsourced).
- **Faults**: cross-legged or side-sit shape (back shin tucked in toward the body rather than pointing back); either knee far from 90; trunk rounding into a C to get the head down; collapsing sideways onto the front hip; front knee lifting off the floor as the trunk folds; trunk twisting away from the front shin.
- **Readability**: the leg shape must read as a Z at a glance, and the back line must read as straight. No single view shows both perfectly: from directly in front the Z is clear but the fold foreshortens toward the viewer; from the side the hinge is clear but the legs overlap. A 3/4 front view from slightly above, on the front-leg side, shows the Z on the floor and the trunk tipping. Front and back legs must be distinguishable.
- **Constraints**: none of the three directly; no impact, no shoulder load if the hands rest lightly. Keep the fold unforced. Mid-back: the straight-back hinge avoids flexing T4-T8 under load.
- **Differs from canon**: none of substance. Cleveland Clinic treats the fold as the advanced option on a held stretch [F]; one outlet warns that leaning forward reduces the front-hip stretch [S]. Will uses a rhythmic fold-and-return rather than a static hold.
- **Checklist**
  1. Are both legs in the same place, on the floor, in every frame?
  2. Is the front shin across the body and the back shin pointing straight behind in every frame?
  3. Are both knees bent to about a right angle in every frame?
  4. Is there a frame with the trunk upright?
  5. Is there a frame with the trunk clearly tipped forward over the front shin?
  6. Is the back a straight line from pelvis to head in every frame?
  7. Does the trunk stay centred over the front shin (no sideways collapse or twist)?
- **Sources**: https://health.clevelandclinic.org/90-90-stretch/ [F]; https://theprehabguys.com/vimeo-video/90-90-hip-stretch/ [F]; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8167346 (hip rotation norms) [S].
