# Daily 13 — acceptance rubrics for the movement demonstrations

**Method.** Each rubric starts from Will's spec in `docs/movement-reference.md` ("User-specific constraints", "Daily 13" and its hard constraints) and is then checked against outside form sources found by web search on 2026-11-12. Where the two differ, Will's spec is the rubric and the difference is recorded under "Differs from canon".
**Source quality.** Strongest: peer-reviewed papers (Myer 2014 squat, Willcox & Burden 2013 clam, McGill 2009 spine stabilization, Fenwick 2009 rows, McKenzie 2022 dips, Ekstrom 2003/2007 EMG) and the ACE exercise library. Weaker: coaching sites (StrengthLog, StrongFirst, Prehab Guys), used for step order only.
**How far each source was read.** Tags after each URL: *[read]* = page text opened and checked; *[abstract]* = only the abstract or a search-result summary was seen, so numbers attributed to it are second-hand; *[not opened]* = URL confirmed to exist, content not checked.
**Unsourced claims** are marked "(unsourced, from Will's spec)". Nothing here proposes changing what an exercise is.
**Reading the checklists.** Eight stills at phases 0, 0.125 … 0.875 of one cycle. Unless stated, phase 0 is the start position and phase 0.5 the far end of the rep. Descriptions are of the human body, not of any rendering.

## 1. Deep squats (bodyweight)

- **Must-see positions**
  - *Top:* standing tall, feet about shoulder-width, toes slightly out, whole foot on the floor, arms wherever they balance the body.
  - *Bottom:* hips well below the knees, nearly sitting on the heels; heels still down; knees forward over the toes and in line with the second toe; trunk leaning forward by the same amount as the shins.
- **Path:** hips move back first, then down; hips and knees then fold together. Feet never move. Shins and trunk tilt forward at the same rate, so at the midpoint (thighs about 45° above horizontal) the two lines are still parallel. On the way up, hips and shoulders rise at the same speed.
- **Angles that matter:** trunk parallel to tibia throughout (Myer 2014, criteria 3 and 6: "Trunk is parallel to tibia", "Tibias are parallel to an upright torso"). Entire foot stays in contact (Myer criterion 7). Ankle closes from about 90° to about 65°, i.e. about 25° of dorsiflexion (unsourced, from Will's spec; the one literature figure found for a heels-down full squat is about 35°, Hemmerich 2006, seen second-hand only). Toe-out small: Myer says no more than 10°.
- **Faults:** heels lifting; trunk folding forward faster than the shins incline (chest toward thighs); lower back rounding at the bottom; knees caving inside the feet; hips shooting up before the shoulders.
- **Readability:** the hip–knee–ankle zigzag and the trunk line carry it. Pure side view (profile) shows depth, heel contact and the trunk/shin parallel; a front view hides all three. Left and right legs overlap in profile, which is acceptable here because the movement is symmetric.
- **Constraints:** none triggered. No jump or bounce out of the bottom (peroneal tendon).
- **Differs from canon:** ACE stops at thighs parallel (or earlier if heels lift or the torso rounds); Myer asks for "at least parallel". Will's version goes to full depth, near the heels. Will's 25° ankle figure is shallower than the ~35° reported for full heels-down squats; the rubric keeps Will's number.
- **Checklist**
  1. Are both heels on the floor in all eight frames?
  2. Do the feet stay in exactly the same place in all eight frames?
  3. At phases 0.125–0.375, do the hips travel backward as well as down?
  4. At phase 0.5, is the hip crease clearly below the top of the knee?
  5. In every frame, are the trunk line and the shin line within about 10° of parallel?
  6. Is the back one straight or gently arched line at the bottom, not a C-curve?
  7. At 0.625–0.875, do hips and shoulders rise together?
- **Sources**
  - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4262933 — Myer, Kushner et al. 2014, back squat assessment *[read]*
  - https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/135/bodyweight-squat/ — ACE bodyweight squat *[read]*
  - https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/jor.20114 — Hemmerich et al. 2006, J Orthop Res 24(4):770–81 *[abstract; the ~35° squat figure was seen only in secondary summaries]*

## 2. Reverse lunges

- **Must-see positions**
  - *Top:* standing tall, feet together under the hips.
  - *Bottom:* one foot stepped straight back about two feet (60 cm), resting on the ball of that foot with the heel up; front foot flat; front shin vertical with knee over ankle; both knees near 90°; back knee 1–2 inches above the floor; trunk upright over the hips.
- **Path:** the front foot never moves. The rear foot leaves the floor, travels back, lands on the ball; then both knees bend and the hips drop straight down, not forward. Return: front leg pushes, rear foot travels forward to rejoin. Midpoint down: rear foot already planted behind, knees part-bent, trunk still vertical.
- **Angles that matter:** both knees about 90°, front shin vertical, back-knee hover 1–2 in (unsourced, from Will's spec). StrengthLog confirms the order (big step back, sink, back knee does not hit the floor, push up with the front leg). Front tibia angle is a real loading variable: a more forward tibia shifts range and moment between the knees (Schütz 2014).
- **Faults:** front knee drifting forward past the toes; back knee touching or slamming the floor; a short step that leaves the front knee sharply bent; trunk pitching forward; front heel lifting; rear foot landing flat.
- **Readability:** the two legs must be told apart at every frame, since one stays and one travels. Profile view shows shin verticality, knee hover and step length; from the front the step collapses to nothing. The rear knee and the floor gap must both be visible at the bottom.
- **Constraints:** step, never hop or switch-jump (peroneal tendon). Rear foot lands softly.
- **Differs from canon:** many current sources allow the front shin to lean 10–15° forward and some accept a light knee tap; Will's spec is a vertical shin and a hover that never touches.
- **Checklist**
  1. Does the front foot stay flat and in the same place in all eight frames?
  2. Is the rear foot visibly behind the body by phase 0.25, and back beside the front foot by the end of the cycle?
  3. At phase 0.5, is the front shin vertical (knee directly above ankle)?
  4. At phase 0.5, is there a visible gap between back knee and floor?
  5. At phase 0.5, are both knees close to right angles?
  6. Is the trunk upright (within about 10° of vertical) in every frame?
  7. Is the rear foot on its ball, heel up, whenever it is behind?
- **Sources**
  - https://www.strengthlog.com/reverse-body-weight-lunges/ — StrengthLog *[read]*
  - https://www.sciencegate.app/document/10.1123/jab.2013-0175 — Schütz et al. 2014, split-squat joint angles vs tibia angle *[abstract]*
  - https://health.clevelandclinic.org/lunges-muscles-worked — Cleveland Clinic lunge overview *[not opened]*

## 3. Dead bug

- **Must-see positions**
  - *Tabletop:* on the back, hips and knees each at 90° (thighs vertical, shins horizontal), both arms straight up toward the ceiling over the shoulders, lower back flat on the floor, head down.
  - *Reach:* one arm lowered overhead to just above the floor; the opposite leg straight and long with the heel hovering just above the floor; the other arm still vertical and the other leg still at 90/90.
- **Path:** arm and opposite leg move away from each other at the same time and return at the same time. Head, ribs, pelvis and the two non-moving limbs do not move at all. Midpoint: moving arm about 45° past vertical toward overhead, moving knee about half-straightened with the thigh about 45° from vertical. The limb pair alternates in real practice (the app shows one pair, then swaps).
- **Angles that matter:** knees over hips bent to 90°, elbows/hands over shoulders at the start (ACE). The gap under the lower back does not change during the reach (unsourced, from Will's spec; ACE says hips and ribcage must not move).
- **Faults:** lower back lifting off the floor as the leg extends; lower ribs flaring up as the arm goes overhead; same-side arm and leg moving; heel or hand resting on the floor; the "still" leg drifting toward the chest; head lifting.
- **Readability:** the contralateral pairing is the whole point, so left and right limbs must be distinguishable in every frame. Side view, slightly raised, shows the floor gap, the hover and the still trunk; from the head or feet the reach foreshortens to nothing.
- **Constraints:** arm reach overhead is unloaded and brief; no interaction.
- **Differs from canon:** ACE has hand and heel "lightly touch the floor (but not rest)"; Will's version hovers and never touches. Will cues the low back pressed flat; McGill's own teaching favours a neutral, braced spine over a flattened one. The visible requirement is the same either way: the trunk does not move.
- **Checklist**
  1. At phase 0, are both thighs vertical, both shins horizontal and both arms vertical?
  2. Are the moving arm and moving leg on opposite sides of the body?
  3. At phase 0.5, is the moving leg straight with the heel off the floor?
  4. At phase 0.5, is the moving hand overhead and off the floor?
  5. Do the non-moving arm and leg hold their start position in all eight frames?
  6. Is the trunk outline (shoulders, ribs, pelvis) identical in all eight frames?
  7. At 0.25 and 0.75, are arm and leg each about halfway, moving in step?
- **Sources**
  - https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/147/supine-dead-bug/ — ACE supine dead bug *[abstract: search summary of the page]*
  - https://www.strengthlog.com/?p=19615 — StrengthLog dead bug *[not opened]*
  - No McGill or Physiopedia page dedicated to the dead bug was found.

## 4. Glute bridge

- **Must-see positions**
  - *Down:* on the back, knees bent, feet flat and hip-width, heels a short distance from the buttocks, arms on the floor by the sides, pelvis on the floor.
  - *Top:* hips lifted until knee, hip and shoulder form one straight line; shins near vertical; shoulders, head and arms still on the floor; feet flat.
- **Path:** pelvis tucks slightly first (low back flattens), then the hips rise by pushing through the heels. Feet, shoulders and head are fixed; the knees stay over the ankles and do not swing fore-aft. Midpoint: hips about half height, trunk a straight ramp from shoulders to pelvis, no arch.
- **Angles that matter:** knee–hip–shoulder angle above 165° at the top, measured on the same side (unsourced, from Will's spec). ACE: lift only as high as the back stays neutral, "don't push your hips as high as possible". Glute activity is higher with the knees bent to 90° or more than with the feet further away (bridge EMG studies comparing 40–120° of knee flexion).
- **Faults:** ribs and belly pushed higher than the knee–shoulder line (lumbar arch); heels lifting, weight on toes; feet so far out that the knees are open past 90° and the hamstrings take over; knees falling in or out; shoulders or head lifting; hips stopping short so the body stays a sagging V.
- **Readability:** the pelvis carries it. Side view at floor level shows the straight line at the top and any arch above it. The near leg must not hide the hip.
- **Constraints:** none triggered; arms rest, no pressing.
- **Checklist**
  1. Are both feet flat, heels down, in the same place in all eight frames?
  2. Do shoulders and head stay on the floor in all eight frames?
  3. At phase 0, is the pelvis on the floor?
  4. At phase 0.5, do knee, hip and shoulder lie on one straight line?
  5. At phase 0.5, is the belly/ribcage no higher than that line?
  6. At phase 0.5, are the shins roughly vertical?
  7. At 0.25 and 0.75, is the trunk a straight ramp, not a banana?
- **Sources**
  - https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/49/glute-bridge/ — ACE glute bridge *[read]*
  - https://data.zhenzhi.com/details/1/879028846 — Ekstrom, Donatelli, Carp 2007, JOSPT 37:754–762, doi 10.2519/jospt.2007.2471 (bridge, side bridge, quadruped arm/leg lift) *[abstract]*
  - https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO202122162153681.page — bridge EMG, 120° vs 60° knee flexion *[abstract]*

## 5. Side-lying clamshell

- **Must-see positions**
  - *Closed:* lying on one side, head on the lower arm, hips stacked one above the other, hips bent and knees bent so the feet are in line with the spine, knees together, heels together.
  - *Open:* top knee lifted away from the bottom knee, heels still touching, pelvis still stacked, bottom leg still on the floor.
- **Path:** only the top thigh moves, rotating outward about the hip with the heels as the hinge. Trunk, pelvis, bottom leg and both feet stay put. The lift is small and slow down. Midpoint: knees a hand-width apart, top hip bone still directly above the bottom one.
- **Angles that matter:** hips flexed 45–60° is the usual prescription; gluteus medius activity was highest at 60° of hip flexion, and both glutes worked harder with the pelvis neutral than rolled back (Willcox & Burden 2013). Range is whatever the hip allows before the pelvis starts to roll, typically a modest opening, not vertical.
- **Faults:** top hip rolling backward to make the knee go higher; heels separating; top foot lifting with the knee (that is a leg raise); trunk rocking; knee opening to vertical, which is only possible by rolling the pelvis.
- **Readability:** the top knee carries it and must be clearly separate from the bottom knee, with the heel contact point visible. View from the front of the body (looking at the belly and knees), slightly raised, so the opening is across the screen and the two pelvis points can be seen stacked. From behind or from the feet the opening is hidden.
- **Constraints:** none triggered.
- **Checklist**
  1. Are the heels touching in all eight frames?
  2. Does the bottom leg stay on the floor, unmoving, in all eight frames?
  3. At phase 0, are the knees together?
  4. At phase 0.5, is there a clear gap between the knees?
  5. Is the pelvis outline in the same place and orientation in all eight frames (no roll back)?
  6. Do head, shoulders and trunk stay still in all eight frames?
  7. Are the hips bent (thighs forward of the trunk line), not straight?
- **Sources**
  - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23160432/ — Willcox & Burden 2013, JOSPT 43(5):325–331 *[abstract]*
  - https://mikereinold.com/the-hip-external-rotation-clamshell-exercise/ — Reinold, clinical commentary on clam hip angle *[not opened]*
  - No ACE or Physiopedia clamshell page was found.

## 6. Bird dog

- **Must-see positions**
  - *Table:* hands under shoulders, knees under hips, back flat and level, head in line with the spine, eyes to the floor.
  - *Reach:* one arm straight forward at shoulder height and the opposite leg straight back at hip height, forming one long horizontal line from fingertips to heel; the other hand and knee carry the weight; back unchanged.
- **Path:** arm and opposite leg leave the floor together, extend together, return together. Spine, pelvis, supporting hand and supporting knee/shin do not move; hips stay level and square to the floor. Midpoint: hand and foot just off the floor, elbow and knee part-bent, both sliding outward low, not swinging up.
- **Angles that matter:** arm and leg finish horizontal, not above the back line (McGill; ACE Big Three article warns against raising the leg high enough to arch the low back). Thighs and arms of the supporting side stay perpendicular to the floor (Physiopedia Core Stability).
- **Faults:** leg lifted above hip height with the low back arching; pelvis tipping or rotating open toward the lifted leg; trunk shifting sideways over the supporting knee; head craned up; same-side arm and leg; back rounding as the limbs return.
- **Readability:** the long diagonal pair carries it. Side view, slightly toward the front, shows the horizontal line and the level back; the reaching arm and leg must be distinguishable from the supporting pair, and it must be obvious they are opposite sides.
- **Constraints:** the supporting arm is straight under the shoulder with body weight only, not a press below parallel; acceptable under the AC rule. Reach is forward at shoulder height, never above.
- **Differs from canon:** McGill prescribes 8–10 second holds with a fist and a braced "sweep" between reps; the app shows continuous 4-second reps. Geometry is the same.
- **Checklist**
  1. At phase 0, are hands under shoulders and knees under hips?
  2. Are the lifted arm and lifted leg on opposite sides?
  3. At phase 0.5, is the hand at shoulder height (not above)?
  4. At phase 0.5, is the foot at hip height (not above)?
  5. At phase 0.5, are the reaching arm and leg both straight?
  6. Is the back line identical in all eight frames?
  7. Do the supporting hand and knee stay in the same place in all eight frames?
  8. Is the head in line with the spine throughout?
- **Sources**
  - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19154838/ — McGill & Karpowicz 2009, Arch Phys Med Rehabil 90(1):118–26 *[abstract]*
  - https://www.acefitness.org/resources/pros/expert-articles/7077/low-back-exercises-stuart-mcgill-s-big-three/ — ACE on McGill's Big Three *[abstract: search summary]*
  - https://www.health.harvard.edu/healthy-aging-and-longevity/three-moves-for-better-spine-health — Harvard Health *[abstract: search summary]*
  - https://www.physio-pedia.com/Core_Stability — Physiopedia *[abstract: search summary]*

## 7. Prone Y-T-W raise

- **Must-see positions** (cycle is Y → T → W → T → Y)
  - *Y (phase 0):* face down, forehead toward the floor, legs long, arms straight overhead and angled out to make a Y with the trunk, thumbs up, hands hovering just off the floor.
  - *T (phases 0.25 and 0.75):* arms straight out to the sides at shoulder level, level with the back.
  - *W (phase 0.5):* elbows bent and drawn down toward the ribs, hands beside the head/shoulders, upper arms angled down and out, shoulder blades pulled together and down.
- **Path:** the arms sweep in the plane of the floor, staying just above it the whole time; the lift is small. Chest, head and legs stay down and still. Between Y and T the straight arms swing outward; between T and W the elbows bend and slide toward the ribs.
- **Angles that matter:** Y is roughly 120–145° of arm elevation, "in line with the lower trapezius fibres" (Ekstrom 2003; ~145° is the figure quoted for it in later papers). T is 90° of abduction. W has elbows bent near 90° (unsourced coaching convention). Hand height: a few centimetres off the floor (unsourced, from Will's spec).
- **Faults:** shoulders shrugged toward the ears; chest and head lifting, low back arching to gain height; hands lifted high; hands or elbows dropping onto, or through, the floor; arms narrowing to an I so the Y is lost.
- **Readability:** the arms carry it and both must be visible. The three shapes spread in the floor plane, so they read best from above or from above the head; a low side view flattens the spread and shows mainly a long arm (Y) versus a short folded arm (W). Will has fixed a side-on view for the prone read and accepts that T is carried by the cue text; do not fail the animation for a weak T from that view.
- **Constraints:** arms stay level with or in front of the body plane, hands never behind the neck or head (AC rule); in W the hands stay beside the head, elbows not pulled above the back. Unloaded. Thumbs-up is a neutral forearm, not supinated pulling.
- **Checklist**
  1. Is the body face down with chest and legs on the floor in all eight frames?
  2. At phase 0, are both arms straight and overhead, clearly either side of the head?
  3. At phase 0.5, are both elbows bent with the hands near shoulder/head level?
  4. Is the arm outline visibly longer at phase 0 than at phase 0.5?
  5. Are the hands off the floor, and above it, in all eight frames?
  6. Does the head stay down (no craning) in all eight frames?
  7. Are the shoulders away from the ears at phase 0.5?
- **Sources**
  - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12774999/ — Ekstrom, Donatelli, Soderberg 2003, JOSPT, trapezius and serratus EMG *[abstract; secondary papers disagree on whether its prone overhead raise peaked middle or lower trapezius]*
  - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2953285 — review citing prone scapular exercises *[not opened]*
  - No reputable source was found for the combined Y-T-W sequence as one drill; the sequence and W shape are from Will's spec and common coaching use.

## 8. Side plank (forearm)

- **Must-see positions**
  - *Line:* body sideways to the floor, supported on one forearm and the side of the bottom foot; elbow directly under the shoulder, forearm flat on the floor pointing forward; feet stacked; top arm along the body; ear, shoulder, hip and ankle on one straight line.
  - *Sag:* same supports, pelvis dropped toward the floor so the body makes a shallow V; shoulder still over the elbow.
- **Path:** only the pelvis travels, down and up. Forearm, elbow, supporting shoulder and feet stay where they are; the body pivots about them. The return stops at the straight line and does not go past it. Midpoint: a slight sag, half the depth.
- **Angles that matter:** shoulder–hip–ankle angle above about 168° in the line position; pelvis drop small but visible (unsourced, from Will's spec). Elbow under shoulder, feet stacked or staggered, straight line shoulder to ankles (ACE plank variations; Prehab Guys). Rib cage locked to the pelvis so the trunk does not twist (McGill 2009).
- **Faults:** elbow ahead of or outside the shoulder; hips hiked above the line (pike); hips drifting backward so the body folds at the waist; top shoulder rolling toward the floor; head hanging; feet sliding.
- **Readability:** the pelvis against the straight line carries it. View from the front of the body (chest toward the viewer), camera low, so the line and the sag are across the screen and the forearm under the shoulder is visible. Viewed from the head or feet, the sag is invisible.
- **Constraints:** forearm support, not straight-arm (AC joint). The app starts on the right forearm; the supporting upper arm is vertical and load is compressive, not a press below parallel. Top arm rests on the body, not overhead.
- **Differs from canon:** McGill's side bridge is a static hold (8–10 s). Will's version is slow hip dips between the line and a small sag, at a deliberately submaximal dose.
- **Checklist**
  1. Is the elbow directly under the shoulder in all eight frames?
  2. Is the forearm flat on the floor and unmoved in all eight frames?
  3. Are the feet stacked and unmoved in all eight frames?
  4. At phase 0, do head, shoulder, hip and ankle make one straight line?
  5. At phase 0.5, is the hip visibly below that line?
  6. Does the hip never rise above the line in any frame?
  7. Does the supporting shoulder stay at the same height in all eight frames?
- **Sources**
  - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19154838/ — McGill & Karpowicz 2009 *[abstract]*
  - https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/blog/6327/plank-variations-5-plank-variations-to-strengthen-your-core/ — ACE plank variations *[abstract: search summary]*
  - https://library.theprehabguys.com/vimeo-video/side-plank/ — Prehab Guys side plank *[abstract: search summary]*
  - https://muschealth.org/content-hub/News/2022/12/20/the-big-three — MUSC Health on the Big Three *[not opened]*

## 9. Plank hold (forearm)

- **Must-see positions**
  - *The hold (only position):* face down, supported on both forearms and the toes; elbows directly under the shoulders, forearms flat and parallel, palms down; legs straight; ear, shoulder, hip, knee and ankle on one straight line; gaze at the floor between the hands.
- **Path:** nothing travels. The only permitted motion is breathing: a very small rise and fall of the rib cage. Upper back stays slightly broad (pushing the floor away), not sunk between the shoulder blades. Any frame should be indistinguishable from any other at a glance.
- **Angles that matter:** shoulders stacked over elbows; no sag in the low back, no hiked hips, no bent knees (ACE front plank). Straight line head to heels (ACE forearm plank article).
- **Faults:** hips sagging so the low back hangs; hips piked above the shoulder–ankle line; head dropped or craned up; shoulders drifting behind the elbows (body pushed back); chest sinking between the shoulder blades; knees bending.
- **Readability:** the whole body line carries it, so the full length head to heels must be in frame. Pure side view at about body height shows sag and pike best; any view from the front shortens the line.
- **Constraints:** forearm plank chosen over straight-arm for the right AC joint. Elbows under the shoulders, not wide.
- **Checklist**
  1. Are the elbows directly under the shoulders in all eight frames?
  2. Do shoulder, hip and ankle lie on one straight line in all eight frames?
  3. Are the knees straight in all eight frames?
  4. Is the head in line with the spine in all eight frames?
  5. Do the forearms and toes stay in the same place in all eight frames?
  6. Is the difference between any two frames small enough to read as a hold, with no visible hip drift?
- **Sources**
  - https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/32/front-plank — ACE front plank *[abstract: search summary]*
  - https://data.zhenzhi.com/details/1/879028846 — Ekstrom et al. 2007 (prone bridge among the nine exercises) *[abstract]*
  - The "push the floor away" serratus cue is (unsourced, from Will's spec).

## 10. Dumbbell bent-over row (neutral grip, two dumbbells)

- **Must-see positions**
  - *Hang:* feet planted about hip-width, knees soft, hips pushed back, trunk hinged forward about 45–60° with a flat back, head in line; arms straight down from the shoulders, a dumbbell in each hand, palms facing each other.
  - *Top:* same trunk angle; elbows bent and driven up and back past the trunk, close to the sides; hands and dumbbells at the lower ribs; elbows higher than the hands; shoulder blades drawn back.
- **Path:** only the arms move. Elbows lead, travelling up and back toward the hips; the dumbbells rise in a near-vertical line and stay under the elbows. Trunk angle, hips, knees and feet do not change. Midpoint: elbows level with the trunk, forearms still vertical, dumbbells about halfway. Lowering returns to a full straight-arm hang.
- **Angles that matter:** trunk 45–60° of forward hinge (Will's spec; ACE's barbell article gives roughly 45–90° from vertical, other coaching sources 45° to near horizontal). Chest height constant through the pull (Will's spec). The bent-over row loads the spine more than other rows and demands high trunk stiffness, so a held, neutral back is the standard (Fenwick, Brown, McGill 2009).
- **Faults:** trunk rising and falling with each rep (heave); back rounding; elbows flaring wide to the sides; hands curling up ahead of the elbows; dumbbells stopping well short of the ribs; palms turning forward/up; shoulders shrugged to the ears.
- **Readability:** elbows and dumbbells carry it, and the dumbbells must stay visibly in the hands. A side view shows the hinge, the still trunk and the elbow travelling up and back; turn it slightly toward the front so the far arm is not completely hidden and the near arm does not cover the trunk line.
- **Constraints:** neutral grip only, palms facing each other, dumbbell handles pointing front-to-back; never palms-forward/up (distal bicep tendon). Elbows close to the body, not wide (AC joint).
- **Checklist**
  1. Is the trunk at the same forward angle in all eight frames?
  2. Is the back a straight line in all eight frames?
  3. Do the feet and knees stay in the same place in all eight frames?
  4. At phase 0, are both arms straight, hanging below the shoulders?
  5. At phase 0.5, are the hands at the lower ribs with the elbows above the hands?
  6. At phase 0.5, are the elbows behind/above the line of the back and close to the sides?
  7. Is a dumbbell in each hand in every frame, handles front-to-back?
- **Sources**
  - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19197209/ — Fenwick, Brown, McGill 2009, JSCR 23(2):350–358 *[abstract]*
  - https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/12/bent-over-row/ — ACE bent-over row (barbell) *[abstract: search summary]*
  - https://www.acefitness.org/resources/pros/expert-articles/3802/ace-total-body-barbell-workout/ — ACE, hinge 45–90° from vertical *[abstract: search summary]*
  - Neutral-grip two-dumbbell specifics came from general coaching sites only (e.g. https://www.muscleandstrength.com/exercises/bent-over-dumbbell-row.html *[not opened]*).

## 11. Bench dips (strict partial)

- **Must-see positions**
  - *Top:* back to a bench, hands on its front edge beside the hips about shoulder-width, fingers forward, arms straight; hips just in front of the bench edge; legs out in front, feet on the floor; trunk upright, chest open.
  - *Bottom (hard stop):* elbows bent straight back; upper arms parallel to the floor and no lower, so the shoulders are level with or above the elbows; hips lowered a short distance, back still close to the bench; feet and hands unmoved.
- **Path:** hands and feet are fixed. The elbows bend and point straight backward; hips and trunk travel straight down close to the bench, trunk staying upright. Stop, then press back up to straight arms. The range is shallow. Midpoint: elbows about half-bent, shoulders still clearly above the elbows.
- **Angles that matter:** upper arm never below horizontal at any point in the cycle (Will's spec). Unrestricted bench dips reach about 88° of shoulder extension, about 101% of the lifters' measured maximum, the largest of bench, bar and ring dips; the authors link that to anterior shoulder strain (McKenzie 2022).
- **Faults:** sinking until the shoulders are below the elbows; elbows flaring out sideways; shoulders rolling forward and up toward the ears; hips drifting forward away from the bench; hands sliding; bouncing at the bottom.
- **Readability:** the elbow, and the upper arm's angle against horizontal, carry it. Side view shows the parallel stop, elbows pointing back and the distance from the bench; the upper arm must not be hidden behind the trunk. The bench has to be recognisable as a bench with the hands clearly on its edge.
- **Constraints:** this is the AC-joint movement. No pressing below parallel, hands shoulder-width (not wide), nothing behind the neck. Any frame with the shoulder below the elbow is an automatic fail.
- **Differs from canon:** the common version lowers "as far as comfortable" (StrengthLog) and in practice well past parallel. Will's is a strict partial that stops at upper arms parallel because of his right AC joint separation. The research supports limiting depth; McKenzie's authors go further and advise against routine bench dips.
- **Checklist**
  1. Do the hands stay on the bench edge, in the same place, in all eight frames?
  2. Do the feet stay in the same place in all eight frames?
  3. At phase 0, are the arms straight?
  4. In every frame, is the shoulder level with or above the elbow?
  5. At phase 0.5, is the upper arm about horizontal (not well short of it either)?
  6. Do the elbows point backward, not out to the sides, in every frame?
  7. Is the trunk upright and close to the bench in every frame?
- **Sources**

  - https://www.strengthlog.com/bench-dip/ — StrengthLog bench dip *[read]*

## 12. Dumbbell farmer hold (static)

- **Must-see positions**
  - *The hold (only position):* standing tall, feet about hip-width, knees straight but not locked; a dumbbell in each hand hanging at the sides beside the thighs, arms straight, palms facing the thighs; shoulders level and down; ribs over pelvis; head over shoulders, eyes forward.
- **Path:** nothing travels. Permitted motion is breathing and a barely visible sway. The dumbbells hang still, not swinging, at thigh height with straight arms.
- **Angles that matter:** body vertical from ankle to ear when seen from the side; shoulders level when seen from the front. A carry is defined by holding posture against the load through trunk and hip muscles (NSCA); back straight and shoulders back (StrengthLog).
- **Faults:** shoulders shrugged up toward the ears; leaning back with the ribs flared and the low back arched; leaning to one side or one shoulder dropped; shoulders rounding forward under the weights; elbows bent, curling the load; head pushed forward.
- **Readability:** the vertical body line and the two hanging dumbbells carry it. A three-quarter front view shows both dumbbells, level shoulders and most of the lean-back fault; pure profile hides one dumbbell, pure front hides the lean-back. Both dumbbells must be visible and clearly in the hands.
- **Constraints:** neutral grip by nature (palms in), which suits the bicep tendon rule. Load hangs; no pressing. Standing still, no impact.
- **Differs from canon:** the standard exercise is a farmer's walk or carry. Will's version is a stationary hold; a 2024 study found the carry gives more trunk activation than the hold at matched load and time, which fits the hold's intended low recovery cost.
- **Checklist**
  1. Is the body vertical (ankle, hip, shoulder, ear stacked) in all eight frames?
  2. Is there a dumbbell in each hand, beside the thigh, in all eight frames?
  3. Are both arms straight in all eight frames?
  4. Are the shoulders level and away from the ears in all eight frames?
  5. Do the feet stay planted in all eight frames?
  6. Is the difference between frames small enough to read as a hold?

## 13. Standing posture reset (breathing / rib stack)

- **Must-see positions**
  - *Flared (end of inhale, start):* standing tall, feet hip-width, arms relaxed at the sides; lower front ribs slightly lifted and forward, a little extra arch in the low back.
  - *Stacked (end of exhale):* same height; lower ribs settled down and in so the rib cage sits directly over the pelvis; pelvis neutral; head over shoulders; arms still relaxed.
- **Path:** a slow breath cycle with the exhale longer than the inhale. On the exhale the front of the rib cage drops and draws in slightly; on the inhale the lower ribs widen to the sides and back rather than lifting at the front. Feet, legs, head height and arms do not move. The change is small: a settling, not a bow or a crunch. Midpoint: ribs halfway, still tall.
- **Angles that matter:** no reliable numbers. The described mechanism is that abdominal activity on exhale pulls the lower ribs down and in and reduces lumbar extension and anterior pelvic tilt, restoring the diaphragm's working position (Boyle 2010; the authors state this is untested hypothesis). Coaching sources describe the target as ribs stacked over the pelvis reached by exhaling, not by hunching (StrongFirst).
- **Faults:** "standing straight" by lifting the chest and arching the low back; folding forward or rounding the shoulders on the exhale; tucking the pelvis hard so the knees bend and the body shortens; shoulders rising on the inhale; visible loss of height.
- **Readability:** the front of the rib cage relative to the pelvis carries it, and it is small. Pure profile is the only view that shows it; from the front it is invisible. Because the visible change is slight, breath pacing (sound or text) carries part of the meaning.
- **Constraints:** none triggered. Arms hang; nothing overhead.
- **Checklist**
  1. Is the figure standing with feet planted and arms at the sides in all eight frames?
  2. Is head height the same in all eight frames?
  3. Is the rib cage slightly further forward/up at phase 0 than at phase 0.5?
  4. At phase 0.5, does the rib cage sit directly over the pelvis?
  5. Is the trunk never bowed forward or rounded in any frame?
  6. Do the knees stay straight and the pelvis level in all eight frames?
  7. Does the exhale half of the cycle take longer than the inhale half?
